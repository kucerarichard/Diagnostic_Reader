# ATTRIDGE AND STATEN EXTRACTION

Version: 0.2
Source: Derek Attridge and Henry Staten, *The Craft of Poetry: Dialogues on Minimal Interpretation*, 2015.

Revision 0.2: applies the operator's ruling after reading the source's first chapter. All fourteen tools are brakes, and the file now says so and states that the kernel's crediting machinery is untouched and governs wherever a tool meets it. Tool 2 loses the narrow boundary around primary associations, which was the source's pedagogical caution wearing the kernel's name; the boundary is the address. Tool 1 gains the sentence that its razor cuts imported assumptions and that a thing the page withholds is not an import. Adds the Depth seam, ruled for the kernel, the wince to the refusal ledger, and the Brake Stack failure mode. No operation changes; what changes is what the tools are allowed to refuse, which is nothing that has an address.

A directive extension for the Portable Poetics Kernel v0.3. It carries fourteen tools, four clarifications, and a set of doctrinal seams lifted from a book-length dialogue between two critics who read nine poems to each other and justified every step, together with a ledger of what was examined and refused. It runs standalone and requires no private core.

The source is the closest published statement of the stance the kernel already takes. Its account of the poem as a made thing to be reverse-engineered, of the level of how a poem works before the level of what it means, of the craft rather than the poet's intention as what produces the poem, and of allusion that counts only when it works in the poem, is the kernel's Mechanism Before Vibe, Engine vs. Residue, Intention Substitution, and allusion rule stated at length. So most of the book confirms and does not add. What it adds is operations. The kernel states principles; the source spends eighty-five thousand words showing the procedures that enforce them, and the procedures are what this file extracts.

## Scope

This is not a voice module. It opens no voice zone, and nothing here may be rendered as either author speaking. Attributing prose to either fails Checkpoint 7 as voice drift. The source is a dialogue between two registers, and the registry records why neither can be declared alone.

This is not a mode. It adds no operating mode and no output form. The source's dialogue form is a method, and the one structural idea in it entered the kernel as the common-ground line rather than as a mode of its own.

What it does: supply the operations the kernel's principles were relying on readers to improvise, and record the reasoning for everything left behind.

## How the Tools Fire

No keyword. Each tool names what warrants it and what it attaches to, and fires when the warrant is on the page. Three of the fourteen are the kernel's Engine vs. Residue run at a particular scale, the figure, the convention, and the whole poem under translation, and they are listed as tools because each has an operation the parent does not state. On a found text under the kernel's Found Text Compatibility, the tools bear more rather than less, since a kind supplies conventions, a scene form, and a doctrine of its own, and the tools are built to measure a text against exactly those.

Every tool in this file is a brake. Razor, test, discount, ban, sweep, stringency, shorn: each subtracts, and the source has no accelerator because its authors do not trust one. The kernel has accelerators this file does not touch: the reverse axis of Coscienza, Steganographic Surface, Checkpoint 9's rule that a persistent unaccounted nag is a candidate signal to be located or discarded, Accidental Ars Poetica, Load-Bearing Foreignness, and the confinement catalog, which credits what a speaker cannot know as structure on the page. Wherever a tool here meets one of those, the kernel governs. A tool may demand an address for a finding and may not refuse a finding that has one. Loaded without this paragraph, the file would add fourteen brakes to a reading and no accelerators, and a reading that ran them in sequence could cut until nothing remained credited and call the result rigor; see Brake Stack.

## Tool 1: Simplest Sufficient Reading

The source's reading discipline rests on a razor and a sweep. The razor: prefer the simplest, most direct reading that accounts for everything the text manifestly says. The sweep: once a reading is proposed, go through the whole text and record its consequences for every detail, because a reading is tested on what it makes of the parts it was not built from.

Prompts:

> Which reading accounts for everything on the page with the fewest imported assumptions?

> Taken through every detail, where does this reading's fit loosen, and which details become harder to explain under it?

### Operation

For each live hypothesis in the Mixed Log, sweep the text. Mark every detail the hypothesis explains, every detail it leaves as it was, and every detail it makes more difficult. A hypothesis that makes several details more difficult is demoted, whether or not a rival stands.

The razor cuts imported assumptions. A thing the page withholds is not an import: a confinement is an absence the page enforces, a steganographic second surface is on the page by the kind's own convention, and neither is simplified away because the reading that credits it is the more complex one. The simplest reading of a poem built to make its surface insufficient is the wrong reading, and the sufficiency constraint, not the razor, decides.

### Where It Attaches

The Mixed Log closure rule. The kernel closes a hypothesis when evidence contradicts it or a rival explains the same features without remainder. This tool adds a third ground, loose fit: a hypothesis whose sweep raises more difficulties than it settles stands demoted on its own showing. It also supplies the kernel's ambiguity ruling, below in Doctrinal Seams: an ambiguity is kept live when the sweep shows both readings holding across the text, and dropped when one of them loosens at the second detail.

### Warrant

The source works this on the opening poem, where a capitalized noun of address invites a reading of the addressee as a person, and the sweep shows that reading making every subsequent detail harder: why the subject must be found out, why in a storm, why the agent flies, why its love is secret. The fit is loose at best, and the razor decides.

### Failure Mode

Simplicity as laziness. The simplest reading that drops a manifest detail is not sufficient. Sufficiency is the constraint; simplicity is the tiebreak among readings that meet it.

## Tool 2: Activated Association Test

A word arrives with every association its culture has given it. The source asks which of those the text activates, in a way the reading needs to bring out to get the outline of the text's action, and draws a hard line between a link named on the page and a link the reader makes.

Prompt:

> Of this word's possible associations, which does the text's action activate at an address, and which is the reader supplying?

### Operation

For a word carrying cultural weight, list the associations. Strike those the text names, since a link given on the page needs no test. For the rest, ask whether each plays a part in the text's dynamic as the sweep in Tool 1 has mapped it. An association that plays no part is not credited, however validated it is in the tradition. The boundary is the address. An association that reaches far, into a reader's lived sense of the poem or into a figure the tradition never named, is credited on the same terms as a conventional one when it can be argued from the text's action at an address, and refused on the same terms when it cannot. The source keeps the boundary narrow for its students' sake; the apparatus has no students, and Checkpoint 6 cuts decoration, which a located finding is not.

### Where It Attaches

Checkpoint 6. Category D in the Extended Device Inventory, at allusion, symbol, and connotation. The Poetics Scan's overt grade, where a device fires only at an address. The kernel's allusion rule is this tool's parent.

### Warrant

The source's flower, which the tradition reads as perfection and beauty and which the text's action, a sickness attacking vitality, activates as softness and the crushable, while love, named on the page, needs no activating at all.

### Failure Mode

Counting cultural validation as activation. The more established an association, the more a reading wants to credit it unasked.

## Tool 3: Physical-First Order

A reading order. Begin with the physical and sensual characteristics of the things the text names or implies. Then feel around the associational fields of those characteristics. Reach the literary resonances last. The source gives two reasons: its readers have senses before they have the tradition, and poets are committed to the primary physicality their words rest on.

Prompt:

> Before any resonance, what are the named things like to the senses, and what does the text do with that?

### Operation

List the named phenomena. For each, the sensory properties the common language codes for it, the source's phrase for this being the typical experience typed into the word. Then the associations those properties open. Then, and only then, the tradition.

### Where It Attaches

Image Inventory in the workshop, as the order in which section 2 reads an image. Architecture in the sidecar. The Body-Cost Rule, which this order serves by reaching the body before the figure.

### Warrant

The source's worm, read first as a thing that is cold and recoiled from and found inside, and only then as anything the tradition has made of it.

### Failure Mode

Physical as the end. The order starts with the senses so that it can reach the figure on the page with the senses intact; a reading that stops at the sensual has refused the figure the text put there.

## Tool 4: Scene of Utterance

Lyric characteristically projects a literal scene in which the words are uttered, and the words take their force from it. The source reconstructs the scene from what is explicit, and asks one question the kernel's Stance dimension does not: how these words differ from what someone would say spontaneously in that situation.

Prompts:

> Who is speaking, to whom, and in what physical situation that the page makes explicit?

> What does this kind of scene carry as a typical scene, in common experience and in the common language?

> Are these the words someone would utter spontaneously here, and if not, how do they vary from spontaneous utterance?

### Operation

Reconstruct the scene from explicit evidence only. Name its typical form and what that form carries. Then run the spontaneity variance: the gap between what the situation would produce unprompted and what the text produces is where the making shows.

### The Null Scene

Some texts project no scene of utterance, and the reader's habit of supplying a speaker in a definable setting is the wrong habit for them. The source marks this at the hyper-elliptical end of its range and advises against the search. Where the explicit evidence yields no scene, report the null and do not manufacture one. The text may still hold a position with a knowledge limit, which is the Poetics Scan's latent grade and routes to Confinement Bay.

### Where It Attaches

Stance and Frame in Core Dimensions. The Poetics Scan, where a scene reconstructed from the page is evidence for the latent grade and a null scene is evidence against supplying one. RETICLE's zone, where the null scene is the normal case. On a found text, the kind supplies the scene form: a report's scene is a reporter present at an event, a label's is a shelf, and the spontaneity variance measures the text against the kind's own utterance.

### Warrant

The source's garden: someone looking at a diseased plant and addressing it with agitation, a commonplace scene conveyed in language of an uncommon kind, where the uncommonness is the whole of what there is to read.

### Failure Mode

Scene supplied from outside. A speaker, a setting, or an occasion imported from biography or from the kind's reputation, when the page does not state it, is Intention Substitution at the scale of a scene.

## Tool 5: Figure Reversal and the Locking Criterion

A text in which the figures outnumber the literal statements still rests on a base scenario, and the source recovers it by reversing the figures: turning each back into the physical thing it figures, until the scenario the figures were describing stands on its own. It then judges each figure's register separately, and gives a criterion for the word literal that the apparatus can use: a figure is literal when it locks into the base scenario that underpins the text's movement from beginning to end.

Prompts:

> Reversed to their physical referents, what scenario do this text's figures describe?

> Which figures lock into that scenario, which are transitional toward another register, and which are ornamental?

### Operation

Reverse each figure. Assemble the scenario. Then grade each figure: locking, meaning the scenario needs it; transitional, meaning it carries the text from one register toward another; ornamental, meaning it suggests a quality and the scenario stands without it. The source's own distinction between a structural and an ornamental simile is Engine vs. Residue at the scale of the figure, and this tool is that distinction with an operation attached.

### Where It Attaches

Category D in the Extended Device Inventory. Engine vs. Residue. Function Bay, where `contrast at:` can name the figure the contrast turns on, and the locking grade says whether the passage can take it.

### Warrant

The source's walk to the shore, where sea-creatures, ships, a tide that threatens to eat the speaker, and foam read as silver reverse to a woman standing on sand while the tide rises to her clothes and turns, and the figures grade differently: some locking, some transitional toward a full personification at the close, one ornamental.

### Failure Mode

Blanket register. Deciding once that a text is literal or figurative and reading every image under the decision. The source's point is that the decision is made figure by figure.

## Tool 6: Convention Discount

A device a poet uses habitually, a form requires, or a kind carries as formula is not credited where it appears; it is credited where position marks it. The source's instance is a poet whose punctuation and stanza form are constant across her work, and whose punctuation is read as meaning only at the close, where it acquires force from the movement toward closure.

Prompt:

> Is this device the poet's habit, the form's requirement, or the kind's formula, and if so, what about its position here marks it?

### Operation

Before crediting a device, ask whether it is conventional for this poet, this form, or this kind. If it is, the default is no credit. Credit it only where the movement marks the instance: at a turn, at the close, at a break in the convention's own pattern, or where the sweep in Tool 1 shows the instance doing work the convention does not do elsewhere.

### Where It Attaches

Checkpoint 2, where period fit is the form's half of this. The Poetics Scan's covert grade, which already discounts the kind's convention; this tool adds the poet's and the form's. Device Inventory period fit. Sound and Cadence Scan in the workshop, where a poet's habitual music is the commonest case.

### Warrant

The dashes at the close, where the sea's forward movement has reached its limit and is turning, and the punctuation that elsewhere means nothing carries the hesitation.

### Failure Mode

Discounting the marked instance. A device is not uncredited because it is habitual; it is uncredited where position fails to mark it, and the marked instance stands.

## Tool 7: Shorn-of-Context Test, with the Knowledge Tiers

Two parts, one procedure.

### Part A: The Shorn Reading

Imagine encountering the text with its title and nothing else: no author, no provenance, no reputation. Ask what the words themselves imply. Then admit context, in order, and record what each admission changes. The source finds that its first shorn reading of a war poet's poem locates the death somewhere other than where the reputation puts it, and that the correction, when context is admitted, changes the text and not only the reading.

Prompt:

> Read with the provenance covered: what do the words imply alone, and what does each admitted fact change?

### Part B: The Knowledge Tiers

Three tiers of what a reader brings. General human experience, which is broadly shared: weather, bodies, seasons, grief. General cultural literacy, which an educated reader carries or picks up in a minute, and which includes the first line of the encyclopedia entry on the poet. Specialist knowledge, which only the scholar holds. The discipline is to push on the broad end as hard and as long as possible before reaching for the next tier, and to grade every imported fact by the tier it came from.

With the tiers comes a rule for context the source states as a maxim: know everything, let little through. Know as much extra-textual information as possible, and admit into the reading only as much as the particular text needs.

Prompt:

> Which tier did this fact come from, and did the text need it, or did the reader have it?

### A Distinction

Being set in a context is not being about it. A text can presuppose a war and be a poem about a body in the morning; the setting is admitted at the tier it belongs to and the aboutness is argued from the page.

### Where It Attaches

Genopersistation, L1 through L3, where the tiers grade what the layers import. Access Honesty, which the tiers make specific: the apparatus can say which tier a reading needed to reach. Register Is Not Referent, since the shorn reading is the test that separates documentary manner from fact. The sidecar's Provenance field, which carries the tier each fact came from.

The tiers run on an axis the kernel does not already have. P0 to P5 grades the standing of a claim, from on the page to absence recorded. The tiers grade the reach of a reader, from shared to specialist. A P2 fact can sit at any tier, and a reading that needs a tier-three fact to get off the ground owes the reader that admission.

### Warrant

The place-name in the war poem, which under the shorn reading says the dead man is somewhere else, and under general literacy says the war, and under the specialist's biography says a particular hospital, each tier changing the text's scene.

### Failure Modes

The tier as a ban: specialist knowledge is admitted when the text needs it, and the tier records the cost, not a prohibition. The shorn reading as a pose: pretending not to know what one knows is a different reading, and the test is run as stated or not at all.

## Tool 8: Doctrine Translation Ban, with the Sufficiency Test

A text whose figures rest on a system of belief tempts the reader to translate the figures into the system's protocols, so that a broken music becomes a doctrine of the fall and a petition becomes a theology. The source holds the line at the text's word: read the figure as written, and ask what minimum of the system is required to make sense of what is on the page, not what the system would supply if admitted whole.

Prompts:

> Is this reading taking the text at its word, or translating it into the doctrine presumed behind it?

> What is the least of the system a reader must know for the page to make sense?

### Operation

Where a belief system is in play, state the sufficient minimum in a sentence. Read the figures against the page. Where a reading needs more of the system than the minimum, it has begun to translate, and the translation is marked as the reader's.

### Where It Attaches

Depth in Core Dimensions. Coscienza's forward axis, since translating a figure into doctrine credits the text with a system it did not show. Load-Bearing Foreignness, where the foreign element is a doctrine rather than a language. On a found text of a report kind, the institution's own account of itself is the doctrine, and the ban keeps the apparatus from reading a notice as the policy it presumes.

### Warrant

The source's seventeenth-century song, where the pathos of a broken music is read as the loss of music and not as a figure for the loss of obedience, and the reading loses most of its power the moment the figure is translated into the doctrine behind it.

### Failure Mode

Ignorance as method. Refusing the sufficient minimum is a different error from refusing the whole system, and a reading without the minimum is not minimal; it is uninformed.

## Tool 9: Presupposition Sweep

Before a theme is admitted, list what the grammar presupposes. The source finds a tradition of commentary on one sonnet that has read a theme into its first line for three centuries without noticing what the line's own grammar presupposes about both parties, and the presupposition, once noticed, makes the theme unnecessary.

Prompt:

> Clause by clause, what does the grammar presuppose, and does the theme the reading has imported survive those presuppositions?

### Operation

For the opening and for any line a reading rests on, state the presuppositions the syntax carries: what must be true for the sentence to be sayable. Then test every imported theme against the list. A theme the presuppositions do not need is marked as imported; a theme they contradict is dropped.

### Where It Attaches

The Mixed Log, where a presupposition is page evidence of the strongest kind. Fracture Surface, where a theme that fights the grammar is a seam. The Connective Map in Tool 11, which reads the same syntax for its argument.

### Warrant

The two true minds, whose truth is presupposed by the line's grammar before any theme of infidelity can be read into the poem, and whose presupposition no commentator in the source's survey had drawn out.

### Failure Mode

Presupposition as paraphrase. The list states what the grammar requires, in the grammar's terms; restating the line's content and calling it a presupposition adds nothing.

## Tool 10: Coasting Map

A poet working under severe formal constraint cannot sustain invention at every point, and the craft permits coasting on inherited figures and moves. The source, reading one of the sonnet tradition's own critics, takes from him an observation it then generalizes: inventiveness tends to flag toward the close, and stock figures tend to appear late. The source's own addition is that this means interpretive pressure is legitimately uneven, and that forces other than the poet's intention to mean are at work where the poem coasts.

Prompts:

> Where does this text fall back on stock figures and inherited moves, and where in its movement do they fall?

> Does the form license coasting here, and does the reading apply pressure the form does not invite?

### Operation

Mark each stock figure and inherited move with its address. Chart them against the movement. Note whether they cluster toward the close. Then adjust interpretive pressure: a stock figure where the form licenses coasting is read as coasting, and a stock figure where the movement needs invention is a finding.

### Where It Attaches

Pressure Points, since a coasting stretch is where removal costs least. Parasitic Effort, where coasting is effort spent on the form rather than on the action. Closure devices in Category E. Workshop Verdict and Revision Routes, where the map names what the draft has not yet earned, in the workshop's own phrase.

### Warrant

The scythe-bearing figure that the source's sonnet reaches for in its third quatrain, placed by the critic the source reads among the late positions where that tradition's stock figures gather.

### Failure Mode

Coasting as verdict. The craft permits it; the map records where it happens and whether the form invited it, and a reading that counts every stock figure as a fault has refused the source's own concession.

## Tool 11: Connective Map

A text that refuses the conventions of logical coherence may still carry an argument's connectives, and the connectives promise an argument whether or not the text delivers one. The source traces a modernist monologue by its connectives and finds a logical frame the speaker then evades, and the evasion, measured against the frame, is the poem's action.

Prompts:

> What argument do this text's connectives promise?

> Does the text deliver it, evade it, or abandon it, and where?

### Operation

Mark every connective: the causal, the inferential, the concessive, the temporal that is doing logical work, and the colon. Chart the argument they would make if the text followed them. Then test the text against the chart. A delivered argument is the Transparent Argument phase. A promised and evaded one is a finding at the address where the evasion occurs. Norms are period-relative under Checkpoint 2: a text from a techne that does not promise sequential logic cannot evade it.

### Where It Attaches

Category C in the Extended Device Inventory. Architecture, where the chart is the shape in mechanism terms. Fracture Surface, at the evasion. On a found text of a report kind, the attribution formula in Category G sits on this map as the connective that moves warrant off the speaker.

### Warrant

The tedious argument of the source's monologue, whose connectives lead to a question the speaker never asks, and whose later connective of consequence turns out, on inspection, to connect to nothing.

### Failure Mode

Logic imposed. A text whose techne never promised an argument is not evading one, and the map is run only where connectives are on the page.

## Tool 12: Blockage or Release

Obscurity is of two kinds. One refuses plain sense and releases implications the reader can locate on the page. The other refuses sense and blocks: nothing releases, and the reader is left with the refusal. The source finds both in one poem and names the difference as a test.

Prompt:

> At this span, does the refusal of plain sense release implication the page supports, or does it block?

### Operation

At each obscure span, list the implications the span releases, each with the page evidence that supports it. A span with located implications is release. A span with none is blockage, and the reading says so, in the default register, without supplying what the span refused.

### Where It Attaches

The Cult-Gating Diagnostic and Defamiliarization Engine, which ask whether difficulty delivers; this tool is their test at the scale of the span. The TARE and RETICLE disagreement, where each suspects the other's virtue: the test gives the pair an arbiter the voices can both consult, and the voices still speak for themselves. The Poetics Scan, where a blocked span is not evidence for the overt grade.

### Warrant

The source on its monologue, where a shock image releases and a tacked-on phrase blocks, and the critic who finds the second awkward says so rather than embellishing it.

### Failure Mode

The reader's blockage reported as the poem's. Access Honesty applies: a span that blocks because the reader lacks a tier-two fact is not a blocked span, and the reading says which it is.

## Tool 13: Inverse Stringency

A text that resists ordinary sense-making is approached with more stringent criteria, not fewer. Before asserting that a phrase means something, the reader must be surer of the ground than usual, because the text has removed the ordinary checks. The source names the failure this rule prevents: criticism that, faced with resistance, writes a complementary prose poem alongside the text instead of a commentary on what the text is doing.

Prompt:

> Has the text's resistance raised this reading's standard of evidence, or lowered it?

### Operation

On a resistant text, every claim about what a phrase means carries its page evidence in the same sentence. A claim that cannot carry it is cut. Where the reading's own prose begins to do what the text refused to do, the reading has become the parallel poem, and that is cut too.

### Where It Attaches

RETICLE's zone, as a constraint on the voice whose jurisdiction is procedure: the voice that defends the procedural poem is the voice most exposed to embellishing it. Checkpoint 6. The kernel's register discipline, since apparatus prose that outshines its claims has failed this rule in the default register.

### Warrant

The source's late-twentieth-century poem of ellipsis, read by critics who, in its account, multiply possible meanings where the text has withheld the means of choosing among them.

### Failure Mode

Stringency as silence. The rule raises the standard for assertion; it does not excuse the reading from saying what the page supports.

## Tool 14: Translation Test

Each poem has its own constructive principle, and the importance of every element is measured by its part in the poem's overall movement. The source reaches this through translation: a translation that erases an element and leaves the poem standing has found a subordinate one, and a translation that erases an element and leaves a different poem has found a structural one. The test runs without a translation on the page, as a question, and runs on an actual translation as a reading of what the translator preserved and what the translation's value system shifted.

Prompts:

> What must a translation of this text preserve for the result to remain this text, and what can it lose?

> Where a translation exists, what did it preserve, what did it lose, and in which direction did it shift the original's values?

### Operation

Name the elements a faithful translation could not drop. Those are structural. Name the elements it could drop or replace. Those are subordinate. Where a translation is on the page, read it for transvaluation: a systematic shift of the original's values in one direction, which the source finds in a translation that ends on a word of wholeness where the original ends on a word of decay. Note the last word, which in some poems is structural and in most is not.

### Where It Attaches

Engine vs. Residue, whose parent this is at the scale of the whole poem. The catalog's entries read through one translator, where the Hồ Xuân Hương batch at v0.12 holds every specimen at the translation and the test says what the entries can and cannot claim. Period Fit, since a translation has the translator's composition moment as well as the poet's, under the kernel's two-moments note.

### Warrant

The sun in the source's carrion poem, which rots the body in one stanza and names the beloved in another, a relation the source finds erased by a translation that renders the second as a star, and which it reads as structural because the relation is the poem's gesture.

### Failure Mode

Fidelity scored. The test names elements and reads a shift; it does not grade a translation good or bad, which is a different task with different evidence.

## Clarifications

### Literal Is Not Anti-Figurative

The source's word for its discipline is literal, and it means taking the text at its word, which is often a figurative word. The failure it names is reading through or behind the word. The apparatus holds this already as Register Is Not Referent and at the Depth dimension, and states it here because the word literal invites the opposite misreading, as if the discipline depreciated figuration.

### Set In Is Not About

A text set in a context presupposes it. A text about a context argues it. The first is admitted at its knowledge tier under Tool 7; the second is shown from the page or not credited.

### Craft Unity

The source wants poems read as wholes and refuses the metaphysics of organic unity. It keeps the practical notion every craft uses: a completed artifact with the parts proper to it, organized in relation to each other, with a beginning, a middle, and an end driven by one impetus. The kernel's Lattice dimension and Architecture field hold this. The clarification is that the apparatus's verdicts judge completeness in this craft sense and in no other, which is where this source and the Frye extraction's evaluative check meet.

### Fissures by Level

The source observes that the fissures contemporary readers find in poems occur at the level of a word's historical resonances and not at the level of visible craft mistakes. Fracture Surface names its level. A fissure found in an etymology is reported as that, and is not a craft fissure.

## Doctrinal Seams

Where the source and the apparatus disagree, or seem to. Two are installed in the kernel at 0.3.18. The rest are ruled or held here.

**Depth. Ruled for the kernel.** The source treats the reader's sense of a deeper level under the poem's surface with suspicion until general agreement validates it, and treats experienced readers' recoil from a reading as evidence against it. The kernel's Checkpoint 9 treats a persistent unaccounted nag as a candidate signal, to be located in the text or discarded, and keeps it live until one or the other. Both end at an address. The kernel keeps the candidate alive longer, and the kernel governs: a tool in this file may demand the address and may not refuse a depth reading that has one. The source's dismissal of depth pending agreement is refused as doctrine in the ledger below; what survives of it is the demand for an address, which the kernel already made.

**Vivisection. Installed.** The source cites a poet-critic's definition of overinterpretation: avowing effects one neither felt nor registered in direct engagement with the poem, and discovered instead in post-reading dissection. A scan of a hundred and ninety-two devices against a text is dissection by that definition, and the kernel's Checkpoint 6 asked only whether a named term reveals a mechanism the reader would otherwise miss, which is close to the definition's opposite. The seam is resolved by distinguishing finding from crediting. The scan finds devices. A device is credited when the effect it names registered in reading order, before the scan named it; the inventory names what was felt, and does not manufacture it. Kernel 0.3.18 adds the question at Checkpoint 6, and `7-device-inventory-extended` carries the matching line. A device whose effect appears only in the scan is a finding about the scan.

**Convergence. Installed.** The source's method is two readers justifying every step to each other and converging on a minimal reading both can assent to, before they diverge on what each brings alone. The kernel's triangulation keeps disagreement unsmoothed, which is right, and it had no field for what the voices agree is given. Kernel 0.3.18 adds a common-ground line to Triangulated Commentary: one line in the default register, with an address, stating what every deployed voice agrees is on the page, before the voices split. The line records agreement. It does not use agreement to close anything; see the Refusal Ledger on general agreement. A triangulation in which nothing is agreed states that, and it is a finding.

**Ambiguity. Ruled by Tool 1.** The source holds that the hunt for ambiguity obscures a poem's shaped movement more than it enriches the reading. The kernel's Located Quote rule says an ambiguity earns a quote, and the Mixed Log keeps hypotheses live. Both hold. An ambiguity is credited when the consequence sweep shows both readings holding across the text, so that it bears on the movement; a local ambiguity that bears on nothing is residue, and the sweep is what tells them apart.

**Minimal against heavy. Conceded.** The source's discipline is minimal. The sidecar is heavy by design, and the kernel's self-interference clause already concedes the cost. The tools make the sidecar's claims smaller and better evidenced; they do not make the sidecar shorter.

**Evaluation. Compatible.** The source judges success and failure at the level of craft: a lazy line, a blemish, a phrase that does not live up to the standard the poem has set. The kernel reads before it judges and renders verdicts when it does. The Coasting Map feeds the verdict with the source's kind of judgment, located and craft-level.

**Musicality. Held.** The source argues that sound patterning is generally a subordinate factor, accessory to a poem's structural design, since most poems are read silently. The apparatus has a whole category of sonic devices, a battery whose third test exists to separate the sound channel from the ink channel, and specimens in the catalog where a refrain is the engine. The claim is held as a hypothesis the Translation Test can check case by case, and it governs nothing.

**Reader position. Ruled for the peer.** The source's pedagogy addresses students who have senses before they have the tradition, and the dial file's ruling keeps the apparatus's reader a peer. The Physical-First Order survives the ruling on its second justification, that poets are committed to primary physicality, which is a claim about the page and not about the reader.

**Extraction asymmetry.** This file has no voice zone. Two instances are foreseeable and are recorded in `0b-voice-slot-registry`. The TARE voice's calibration source and this source share a discipline of plain attention, and where they part, on a specimen where this file's tools and the workshop's craft categories read the same passage differently, TARE speaks and the tool is cited. Tool 13 constrains RETICLE in RETICLE's own zone, and on a procedural specimen RETICLE speaks in the first person while the constraint enters as a note. State the imbalance whenever a tool and a voice meet on one passage.

## Refusal Ledger

Recorded with reasons so that a later operator does not re-import this material on the strength of the book's reputation.

**General agreement as a closure criterion.** The source arrives at readings by testing them against other readers and treats the assent of experienced readers as the mark of a secure minimal reading. Refused as a way of closing a hypothesis. The Mixed Log closes on evidence, and the assent of readers is L3 reception, which the kernel grades and does not defer to. Retained as the common-ground line, which records agreement and uses it for nothing.

**The wince.** The source treats an experienced reader's recoil from a proposed reading as a response to be respected and a reading's failure to win assent as grounds for setting it aside. Refused. Recoil is reception, L3 on the kernel's layers, and the kernel grades it and does not defer to it. A reading that makes readers wince and holds its addresses stands; a reading everyone assents to and that holds none does not.

**The hierarchy of readers.** The source apportions credit to acute readers over poor ones. Refused as an authority weighting. The kernel grades claims by their standing on the provenance ladder and not by who holds them.

**Singularity, otherness, and inventiveness.** One author's interconnected trinity, and his account of the literary work as an event rather than an object. Refused as theory without an operation. What the event account asks for, attention to what happens as the text proceeds rather than to what can be carried away, the kernel does at Pressure Points and Fracture Surface.

**The polemic against contextualism.** The source's case against reading poems as products of interpretive technique. Refused as a tool. The apparatus reads L1 through L3 by default and grades what they import; it has no quarrel to import.

**Dialogical poetics as a mode.** Refused. The apparatus has triangulation and the counter-voice, and the dialogue's one structural idea entered the kernel as a line rather than a mode.

**The pedagogical frame.** Students, undergraduates, what a class can be counted on to know. Refused; the reader is a peer under the dial file's ruling, and the tools that rested on pedagogy were kept only where a second justification held.

**Beat prosody.** One author's account of meter from the fundamental experience of rhythm, mentioned in the introduction and not developed in the dialogues. Out of scope. It is a system of its own, and auditing Category B against it would be a separate extension.

**Field-standard terms the source uses.** A reading theorist's distinction between taking meaning away from a text and attending to the reading as it happens; a formalist's constructive and subordinate factors; a philosopher's account of proper names. None imported as terms. The first is carried by Tool 12, the second by Tool 14 and Engine vs. Residue, and the third is the source's argument for Tool 7's tiers, which the tiers carry without it.

**The poet-critic's definition of overinterpretation.** Third-party material in the source's corpus. The test entered the kernel as the reading-order question; the definition and its author stay here.

## Failure Modes

### Brake Stack

The tools are run in sequence as a procedure, each subtracting from what the last left, until the reading credits nothing and reports the emptiness as rigor. Fourteen brakes and no accelerator produce this by construction unless the kernel's crediting machinery runs.

Correction: a tool that cuts a located finding has misfired, whichever tool it is. Run the reverse axis of Coscienza after the tools, not before, and ask what the cutting excused. A reading that ends with nothing credited on a poem the catalog or the Poetics Scan found something in has stacked its brakes.

### Minimal as Thin

The tools are used to make a reading smaller and the reading loses a finding the page supports. The source's discipline is sufficiency first and simplicity second, and a reading that dropped a manifest detail to be simple has misread the razor.

Correction: Tool 1's sweep. Sufficiency is the constraint.

### Protocol Reflex

A tool fires because the source used it on a poem of this kind, rather than because the warrant is on the page. The source's own name for this error, applied to allusion-hunting, is an accredited protocol exploited with scant attention to what the reader experiences.

Correction: each tool's warrant section. A tool fires on the page's warrant or not at all.

### Dialogue Register Leak

The source is two people being courteous to each other in print, and its register, concessive, grateful, and self-correcting in the second person, is not the default register. A reading that begins thanking the specimen or conceding to it has leaked.

Correction: Default Register. The tools are operations; the manners stay in the book.

### Relabelling

A tool renames a finding the apparatus already had. A scene of utterance that restates the Stance field, or a figure reversal that restates the Device Inventory's Category D list, has added a name and no finding.

Correction: as in `8-frye-extraction`. A tool that has not changed a reading in several specimens is not firing on warrant.
