## Calibration Packets

### SC-001-CAL

Specimen handle: Snow Crust
Source unit: SC-001
Function in plugin: foundational accidental ars poetica

#### Diagnostic Function

Snow Crust trains the apparatus to notice a poem whose surface argument fails while the failure becomes the poem's second-order value. The speaker tries to defend bundling as reasonable, economical, and chaste. The proof collapses when the most vivid evidence comes from the floor, the open winter ground, and the socially disowned body.

#### L0 Provenance

- New England broadside, c. 1786.
- Anonymous.
- Public domain.
- Source-bearing calibration specimen for failed-system readings.

#### Architecture

- Ballad meter throughout.
- Genesis frame: Adam and Eve license marriage and bodily warmth.
- Practical frame: bed, sheets, wood, night, rest.
- Moral frame: chaste bundling versus dishonest desire.
- Evidence frame: Kate, Nance, Sue, Ruth, then the unnamed woman on snow crust.
- Closure: statistical shrug that reverses the cautionary structure.

#### Fracture Surface Scan

- The poem claims to defend bundling.
- It accumulates anti-bundling examples.
- The speaker claims reluctance to name the woman, then gives the most memorable details.
- The cautionary example becomes more vivid than the defense.
- The closing couplet turns warning into practical argument.

#### Coscienza

Forward axis:

The speaker should not be credited with full awareness of the poem's global collapse. The poem is not controlled satire. The defense is sincere.

Reverse axis:

The speaker should not be treated as inattentive. He returns to the snow-crust example, hesitates over naming, gives timing, setting, and astrological detail, and lets the example dominate the poem's memory. He knows what he is doing locally even when the full argument escapes him.

#### Device Inventory

- Genesis frame as theological license.
- Catalog of names: Kate, Nance, Sue, Ruth.
- Apophasis: the refusal to name becomes a way of naming.
- Ballad lattice carrying argumentative material.
- Anti-rhetorical statistical close.
- Floor-origin pressure point.

#### Mixed Log

Dominant hypothesis:

The poem is a sincere defense of bundling that fails on its own terms.

Qualified-live hypothesis:

The poem is an accidental ars poetica because its first-order failure produces second-order diagnostic value.

Closed hypothesis:

The poem is a covert satire. Closed because the tone remains sincere and the narrator's affect does not signal satirical control.

#### Genopersistation

L0:

Ballad meter, common measure, folk lattice, rough rhymes, oral-derived cadence.

L1:

Anonymous broadside in the New England bundling controversy. The local practice and its moral policing are load-bearing.

L2:

Broadside print culture and pamphlet circulation. Survival depends on archival capture rather than literary prestige.

L3:

Later preservation as Americana, folklore, and sexuality-history material. Selective Salvage likely keeps bawdy comedy and drops the theological argument.

#### Pressure Points

First pressure point:

> "She was the first that on snow crust / I ever knew to gender"

This cannot be paraphrased without loss. The archaic verb, the winter ground, the open exposure, and the sexual fact carry the diagnostic pressure.

Second pressure point:

> "Perhaps there's more got on the floor / Than any other way."

The couplet converts caution into statistical defense. It is the poem's epistemological collapse.

#### Phase Tensions

Yes. The surface argument defeats itself.

Intentional Glitch:

No. The narrator is not engineering the failure.

Transparent Argument:

No, with an adjacent condition. The engine is visible because the narrator cannot hide it, not because he declares it.

## Source Corpus

### SC-001 Raw Source

Source title: "The Whore on the Snow Crust"
Core handle after first title notice: Snow Crust
Status: public domain
Exposure status inside this plugin: full source retained for private calibration

```text
Adam at first was formed of dust,
As we find of record;
And did receive a wife call'd Eve,
By a creative word.
From Adam's side a crooked bride,
We find complete in form;
Ordained that they in bed might lay
And keep each other warm.
To court indeed they had no need,
She was his wife at first,
And she was made to be his aid
Whose origin was dust.
Though Adam's wife destroyed his life
In manner that is awful,
Yet marriage now we all allow
To be both just and lawful.
And nowadays there is two ways,
Which of the two is right:
To lie between sheets sweet and clean
Or sit up all the night.
But some suppose bundling in cloaths
The good and wise doth vex;
Then let me know which way to go
To court the fairer sex.
Whether they must be hugg'd and buss'd
When sitting up all night;
Or whether they in bed may lay,
Which doth reason invite?
Nature's request is, give me rest,
Our bodies seek repose;
Night is the time, and 'tis no crime
To bundle in our cloaths.
Since in a bed a man and maid
May bundle and be chaste,
It doth no good to burn up wood;
It is a needless waste.
Let coat and shift be turned adrift,
And breeches take their flight;
And honest man and virgin can
Lie quiet all the night;
But if there be dishonesty
Implanted in the mind,
Breeches nor smocks nor scarce padlocks
The rage of lust can bind.
Kate, Nance and Sue proved just and true
Though bundling did practise;
But Ruth beguil'd and proved with child
Who bundling did despise.
Whores will be whores, and on the floors
Where many have been laid,
To set and smoke and ashes poke
Won't keep awake a maid.
Bastards are not at all times got
In feather beds, we know;
The strumpet's oath convinces both
Ofttimes it is not so.
One whorish dame I fear to name
Lest I should give offense,
But in this town she was took down
Not more than eight months since.
She was the first that on snow crust
I ever knew to gender;
I'll hint no more about this whore
For fear I should offend her.
'Twas on the snow when Sol was low
And was in Capricorn,
A child was got and it will not
Be long ere it is born.
So unto those that do oppose
The bundling trade, I say:
Perhaps there's more got on the floor
Than any other way.
```
