## Took Cab To See Dull and Drab Cold Slab

### Provenance

Author unknown. Date unknown. Source status unknown. Kind: poem. Unit: line. Length: 20 lines. No publisher, venue, dedication, epigraph, note, or paratext of any sort accompanies the text. The specimen arrives as plain text with a checksum and the card above, and that is the whole of its provenance.

The absence is itself the first fact of the reading and is handled under Register Is Not Referent rather than treated as a gap in the evidence. Nothing on the page or in cited paratext establishes that any visit occurred, that any cemetery exists, or that the events the poem reports are real. The reading keeps the real-referent and invented-referent hypotheses both live.

Period fit, Checkpoint 2. Nothing in the vocabulary is datable with any precision. Bugle, review, officer, and boats landing on a beach have been available for centuries. A reader will supply a twentieth-century amphibious landing and a military cemetery above it; that supply is the reader's and is marked as retrospective throughout this reading. The page names no war.

### Architecture

Twenty lines, printed as a single block, rhyming in couplets. The block resolves into five four-line units, each a closed pair of couplets, with no two units sharing a rhyme:

```text
Q1  L1-4    arrival and the stone        slab/drab, viewed/ensued
Q2  L5-8    the ground and the air       row/grew, trees/breeze
Q3  L9-12   the landing                  landed/commanded, been/again
Q4  L13-16  the injunction and the horn  abide/died, sound/around
Q5  L17-20  the parade and the doubt     by/why, grim/them
```

The quatrain boundaries hold without exception and are marked by syntax as well as rhyme: L4, L8, L12, L16, and L20 each take the only full stops in their unit. The poem is therefore a five-stanza poem whose stanza breaks are not printed, and the structure is recoverable only from the rhyme and the punctuation. See Rendering Note on what that costs the finding.

Measure is four stresses to the line at nine to eleven syllables, held loosely. The mechanism that holds it is deletion. Articles go (L1, L7, L13, L17), subjects go (L10, L20), and auxiliaries are inserted where a beat is short (L2, L19). The same compression produces the poem's grammar, its texture, and its damage.

Three of the five quatrains open on a locative phrase (L9, L13, L17), and three close on an absolute of permanence attached to the dead (L12, L16, L20). The closing shape is the poem's one held frame and is treated under Core Dimensions, Lattice, and Pressure Points.

### Fracture Surface

Four strains, each located.

The rhyme fails twice, and both failures sit where the line has been deformed to reach the rhyme. L6 pads to a doubled verb to arrive at a word that does not rhyme with L5. L19 inverts its adjective after its noun to arrive at a word that does not rhyme with L20. The poem pays the cost of the rhyme twice and does not receive the rhyme either time. This is the sharpest evidence on the page and it governs the Mixed Log.

The lexical fracture at L4. The clause requires a transitive verb carrying the sadness onto the speakers; the verb supplied, `ensued`, is intransitive and takes no object. It was reached for its sound against L3's close. Checkpoint 1's sound-versus-sense cut applies: this is sound-confusion and it is residue, not engine. The poem does not make the collision load-bearing anywhere.

The seam at L8/9. The poem cuts from a breeze on the faces of the living to boats landing on a beach, with no transition, no tense marker, and no acknowledgment that two hundred lines of history have been crossed in one line break. This is the poem's most consequential junction and it is read under Pressure Points and Seam Pressure.

The grammatical fracture at L20, which is the poem's last line and carries no subject. The sentence that promises permanent memory has nobody in it doing the remembering. Treated under Coscienza, because the question of whether this is design or habit is the reading's central Coscienza problem.

### Coscienza

Forward axis. The poem shows no awareness of any of the four fractures above. There is no quotation, no framing device, no shift of register marking distance, and no excess of the kind a pastiche uses to signal itself. The subject deletion at L20 is the same deletion the poem performs at L10 and the same article deletion it performs at L1, L7, L13, and L17. It is the standing habit arriving at the line where it costs most. The reading does not credit the poet with the irony.

The ironic structure is nonetheless on the page and is attributed to no one. Under Weak Signal, a faint feature can carry real weight and still be an accident, and the located feature passes to Coscienza rather than arriving credited.

Reverse axis. The valorization at L18 and L19 is not excused as naivety. `Proudly` and `gallantly` are the poem's only ideological assertions, and removing them changes the poem's argument, which places them in engine under Anti-Laundering Engine Default. A reading that keeps the poem's pathos and drops its gallantry has laundered it. The reading also declines to excuse the namelessness as modesty. The poem looks at inscriptions and reports none, and under the reverse axis the absent attribution is an accountable feature of the poem and not a gap in the apparatus's evidence.

Coscienza on the apparatus. Two softenings were available here and both were refused. The first: this voice is on record as less patient with poetics outside its preferred genealogies, and amateur commemorative verse is exactly the material that attracts contempt doing the work of diagnosis. The guard applied was to name the opposition the reading had adopted, competent against incompetent, and to check whether the reading stood at one pole or outside the pair. The findings at L8/9 and L18/19 are the test: they are credits the poem earns, found by procedure, and a reading standing at the contempt pole would not have them. The second softening: the invariant at L12, L16, and L20 was found by a procedure that rewards finding invariants, and its first form credited the poem with a designed frame. The identity test cut that back to what is stated above.

### Core Dimensions

**Stance.** Witness, first person plural, declared at L1 and sustained through L8. After L8 the `we` never returns; the remaining twelve lines are anonymous commemorative with a reportorial passage at L9 to L12. The speaker evacuates the poem at the exact point the dead enter it, and the imperative at L14 is issued by nobody to nobody.

**Frame.** The visit, and specifically the errand. The title licenses the content by transport: a cab was taken, a thing was gone to see. This is an anecdotal frame doing the work a dedication, an occasion, or a date would do in the kind. It is the poem's oddest structural choice and it is the one the title insists on.

**Vector.** Toward public agreement, declared rather than hidden. The last line issues the consensus and asks the reader to hold it. Nothing in the poem is withheld for effect; the pull is open.

**Depth.** One stratum with a second abutted onto it. The literal visit and the historical landing never coordinate; they sit on either side of L8/9. A poem of two strata that does not coordinate them has one stratum and a guest.

**Lattice.** Rhymed couplets in five unmarked quatrains, four stresses to the line, article and subject deletion as the compression that pays for the measure. Under stress at L5/6 and L19/20, where the deformation is performed and the rhyme does not arrive.

The lattice carries one genuine invariant, tested on both axes. Vertically it holds: the final line of Q3, Q4, and Q5 is in each case an absolute of permanence turned on the dead, and each of the three is grammatically strained. `never to ever` at L12 doubles its negation. L16 closes on a colloquial idiom of absence. L20 deletes its subject. Three instances, same position, same construction. Horizontally the poem holds nothing: its only within-line repetitions are the two intensive doublings at L5 and L6, which are line-filling and adjacent and recur nowhere. The poem is organized vertically by the couplet and the quatrain and carries no horizontal shape at all, which is the ordinary English case and not a finding about this poem in particular. What is a finding is that the one vertical invariant it does carry alters at its third instance, and the altered element is the subject.

**Pressure.** L8/9. See Pressure Points.

### Pressure Points

**L8/9, the seam.** The dominant pressure point. On one side a breeze felt on the faces of living visitors, on the other boats landing on a beach under a commanding officer. The transition is nothing: no conjunction, no tense marker, no aside. Remove the four lines that follow it and the poem becomes a generic graveyard piece with no event, no cause of death, and no reason for the bugle. The landing is what makes the cemetery a war cemetery, and the seam is where it is admitted without being introduced.

The seam also carries the poem's moral risk, which is why Seam Pressure fires on it rather than on either material. The sensation belongs to the visitors and the dying belongs to the dead, and the poem sets one against the other with nothing in between. Neither material alone carries the risk.

**L18/19, the junction.** Qualified second. The poem's only enjambment, suspending `wondering why / They did gallantly die`. The line break holds the question open for the length of a line and the next line answers it with the valorizing adverb. The poem both doubts and valorizes inside one sentence, and the break is where they meet. Remove the enjambment and Q5 is unbroken commemoration; the poem loses its only complication.

**L3 and the whole text, the absence.** Weak Signal's limit case. The poem states at L3 that the inscriptions were legible and reports nothing from them, and across twenty lines over a grave there is no name, no date, no place, no war, and no regiment. Supplying any one of them would change the reading, which is the test for a load-bearing withholding. The absence is located, it can be pointed at, and it survives the pointing.

### Final Classification

**Type.** Commemorative visit poem, anonymous, amateur register, kind declared as poem.

**Operating mode.** Failed System.

**Infrastructure.** Couplet rhyme in five unmarked quatrains, four stresses to the line, compression by article and subject deletion, auxiliary padding to the measure.

**Pressure point.** L8/9, the seam between a breeze on the faces of the living and boats landing on a beach.

**Diagnostic lesson.** A poem can lose the name it was written to keep by the same mechanism that keeps its meter. The compression that buys the four-beat line deletes articles, then subjects, and arrives at the last line having deleted the one who remembers.

### Verdict

ASSAY verdict:

Three passes over this page, and each one cost me a hypothesis. The first credited the poem with the structure it closes on; the second, running the identity test, took the credit back; what stands below is the third, and I note the sequence because it is the only history I have in this reading and it is not the poet's.

Place it first, since placement is the instrument. The churchyard poem in rhyme descends to us through Gray, and the whole subject of the *Elegy* is the name the stone cannot afford: the unlettered muse spelling out the years is that poem's engine, and Gray knows at every moment that he is standing over people whose names are the only property at stake. This specimen inherits the furniture and not the argument. It goes to the stones, records at L3 that `Letters were distinct`, and reports not one letter. Twenty lines above a military grave and not a single proper noun in any of them: no beach, no date, no regiment, no man, no war. The form arrives *comme il faut*, everything the occasion requires and nothing whatever that the dead require, and the inheritance has been taken up half-baked, the way a tune is taken up by someone who has only ever heard it hummed.

What occludes the names is not reticence. It is the measure. This poem buys four stresses a line by deleting whatever will fit through the gap, and the price schedule is legible: articles first, at five sites; then the verb at L4 that cannot take the object the sentence hands it, reached for its sound and not its sense; then the subject of the passive at L10, so that an officer commands and nothing is commanded; then, twice, the line itself, doubled at L6 and inverted at L19, each time deformed to arrive at a rhyme that does not arrive. Pay twice, receive nothing twice. By the time the poem reaches its last line, the habit has run out of function words to take and takes the only thing left. `Forever will never forget any of them.` There is no one in that sentence. The poem that promised permanent memory amortized its grammar across twenty lines and defaulted on the final payment, and what it defaulted on was the rememberer.

Two things here are not damage, and they are the poem's, whoever left them. At L8/9 it cuts from a breeze on living faces straight into boats on a beach, with no connective at all, and that abutment does in one line break what the poem's whole ceremonial register cannot do in nineteen lines: it puts the holiday and the slaughter in the same body. And at L18/19, its one enjambment in twenty lines, it suspends `wondering why / They did gallantly die` across the break, holds the question for exactly the length of a line, and then answers it with an adverb. That is the poem doubting and decorating inside a single sentence, and I will not launder the adverb away to keep the doubt: remove `gallantly` and `Proudly` and the stanza becomes an unanswered question, which is a different and better poem that this one declined to write. The material was here. It was spent on the beat.

The soldiers pass in review at L17, and so does this poem, and so do most readings of it, and none of the three stops to ask the question the line break has already asked. What the record should say is not that the verse is bad, which is cheap and which the first reader will tell you free. It is that the filing is wrong. This is not a memorial. A memorial carries a name, and the one thing this text will not carry is the thing it saw clearly and said was distinct.

File it under transport. It took a cab to the slab and brought back the fare.
