# Operator note: what came out of the run

The run produced two files on disk. It also produced a reading in a person, which is what the
apparatus is for and which nothing in the run directory would otherwise record. This note is
that, written the same day, after the outputs were read.

It is not a finding, carries no score, and amends neither output.

## The observation

The operator's, unprompted, after reading the treatment:

> the speaker gets a lot of offers doesn't she? never thought of it that way, the tasks of
> navigating all the offers

That is a reading of the poem's first movement as labour. Three things arrive in six lines,
each as a question, each leaving as a verdict. The poem feels busy for thirteen lines because
refusing is work, and the work is the content.

## What it opened

Five things followed from it that neither output states, and they are set down here because
they came out of the exchange rather than out of the apparatus.

**The refusals are graded, not repeated.** The first is turned back on ownership, the second
on authenticity, the third on neither. By the third the poem has stopped supplying a ground
and supplies a cost instead. That is where the imperative mood stops, which is the junction
both outputs identify by punctuation, reached here from the other direction, by asking what
the refusals are refusing on.

**The smallest courier.** Thinking about the kinds of small message a person actually
receives put a note on a sticky square beside the poem's first delivery. A snail's trail on a
leaf is a mark left on a small flat surface by someone already gone, which is what a note on
a sticky square is. The poem refuses it on the ground that it is not the speaker's, which is
an odd thing to say of a message rather than a parcel.

**Nobody sends.** The title names carriers. A courier is by definition someone else's hands,
and no line in the poem says who dispatched any of it. The speaker dispatches nothing either.
She receives, inspects, refuses, and the poem's whole economy is one-directional. The
reading's verdict reaches the same shape by a different route: an inspection whose warrant is
never produced.

**The last line cannot be placed.** `Love, love, my season` is either a delivery that goes
unrefused or the first thing the speaker says on her own account, and the poem does not
settle which. That is Tool 5's finding arriving from the reader's side: the closure is the
reader's decision, and here there is not even enough on the page to decide what kind of
sentence it is.

**Reflection is in the poem, and it is breaking.** Noticed when this file was being named.
The word itself does not appear: zero hits for `reflect` across the thirteen lines. The thing
appears twice, in consecutive lines, at L11 and L12, as a disturbance in mirrors and then as
the sea shattering its grey one, which is the sea's own reflection. The poem's penultimate
image is a reflection coming apart.

That sits inside the structure the rest of this note describes. L11 and L12 are deep in the
unrefused half. The three things the speaker turns back are all tokens, things standing in
for something else: a word, a substance sealed in a tin, a ring with the sun in it. After L6
the poem stops being handed representations and starts being handed the things themselves,
and among those is a mirror failing. She refuses what represents; what arrives unrefused
includes representation itself coming apart. The poem's only dash, a doubled em-dash, closes
L12 and hands over directly to the last line, so whatever `Love, love, my season` is, it comes straight out
of a shattered reflection.

One correction the exchange produced and the poem insists on: the poem never says who is
receiving. No name, no pronoun, no disclosed self. The operator said "she", as any reader
would with this author on the cover, and the page supplies nothing to support it. That
absence is the reading's central claim about why the poem was mis-filed, met here by accident.

## Why it is in the run directory

The Martino thread in this apparatus was built for a reader with a trained ear in one art and
none in another. The honest record of this run is that the handle did not come from the
letter-to-note mapping, which did not fire here and would have told the reading nothing. It
came from reading the poem after the apparatus had laid its structure out, and then noticing
something in it that the apparatus had not said.

That is a result worth keeping, and it is the only kind this suite has no field for.

## Provenance

The observation quoted above is the operator's. The four elaborations came out of the
conversation that followed and are not attributable to either output. The operator's own
account of what they have and have not sent and received is left out of this file
deliberately; it is theirs and this repository is public. The insight it produced is kept.
