## Architecture

Six couplets and a single closing line. No rhyme scheme. No metrical contract held across the poem; line length runs from four words to eleven.

The structural fact that organizes everything else is a frame that runs for three couplets and then stops. In S1, S2, and S3 the first line presents an object and ends on a question mark, and the second line refuses it. In S4, S5, and S6 the question mark is gone, the refusal is gone, and the objects keep arriving. L13 arrives with neither mark nor refusal and with the only direct address in the poem.

Grammatically the second half is one long run of noun phrases without a governing main clause, stanza breaks crossed twice by syntax. The apparatus that would have processed those phrases has been withdrawn, and nothing replaces it.

## Fracture Surface

The fracture is the S3/S4 turn, at the junction L6/L7. Two features leave the poem at the same point: the interrogative mark and the imperative mood. The poem does not mark their departure, acknowledge it, or compensate for it.

A second strain sits at L6 itself, inside the frame rather than at its edge. S1 and S2 refuse in the imperative, addressed to a second person. S3 refuses in a nominal fragment addressed to nobody, and the refusal becomes a verdict with no verb and no recipient. The frame is already failing one couplet before it stops.

A third, smaller strain: L12 closes on a doubled long dash, leaving the final couplet syntactically unfinished, and L13 does not complete it. The dash opens and the next line changes subject.

## Core Dimensions

**Stance.** An inspector of deliveries. The speaker holds the authority to refuse an item and exercises it three times without explaining where the authority comes from. The posture is neither confessional nor reportorial; it is the posture of someone to whom things are brought for approval. At L13 the posture changes to direct address and is not explained either.

**Frame.** Riddle and emblem. Each of the first three couplets presents an object for decoding, which is the riddle's contract, and then declines to decode it, which is the breach. The title supplies the delivery frame and the agents; nothing in the body names either.

**Vector.** Toward refusal, declared openly in the imperative. The pull reverses or stops after L6, and the second pull is not declared. The undeclared pull is the more forceful of the two precisely because the poem has spent six lines training the reader to expect a verdict.

**Depth.** Two strata coordinate, with a third thin. The literal stratum is a sequence of presented objects. The epistemic stratum tests each for ownership and authenticity. A seasonal or meteorological stratum runs from L7 to L13 and is thin, since it supplies images and carries no argument of its own until the last line claims it.

**Lattice.** The scaffold is the offer-and-refusal couplet, not meter and not rhyme. It holds for three units, degrades at the third, and is gone from the fourth. The lattice is under stress at exactly one place, S3, and absent thereafter, which makes this a poem held up by a structure that leaves before the poem does.

**Pressure.** The junction L6/L7. Removing S4 through S6 leaves a competent three-couplet refusal poem that means considerably less. Removing L13 leaves the images with no claimant. Removing the junction is not possible, which is what makes it the pressure point: the poem's cost is concentrated in a transition rather than in a line.

## Triangulated Commentary

Common ground, in the default register, with an address. Both deployed voices agree on one thing given on the page: the interrogative mark and the imperative mood leave the poem together at the S3/S4 junction, and nothing from L7 to L13 is refused. The disagreement below is mapped against that.

**ASSAY:**

Take the thing at its filed position first. A poem of thirteen lines, in a posthumous volume whose selection and sequence were somebody else's work, and the critical tradition that grew up around the volume has for decades had almost nothing to say about it, which is not an oversight so much as a sorting decision executed at scale: the commentary wanted a life, this poem declines to supply one, and the drawer it was put in has a label it does not answer to. What is on the page is an inspector. Three objects are brought, three objects are turned back, and the refusals are graded, first on title and then on authenticity, in the flat idiom of a customs desk rather than of a confession. Then the desk closes. From L7 the items keep coming and nobody signs for anything.

The machinery that mis-filed it is nameable and it is cheap to name: an edition whose arrangement the poet did not make, an anthology practice that amortizes a poet into four reproducible poems, and a biographical criticism that underwrites every reading with a life and therefore cannot process a speaker who has none. Those three laundered a procedure poem into a confession with the names missing. The friable part of the received account is exactly here. Take away the biography and the famous four stand up fine; take away the biography and this poem improves, which is a test the received account cannot survive and has never been made to sit for.

So the reassignment. This is not a lyric that withholds its subject. It is an inspection whose warrant is never produced, and its proper company is the riddle and the emblem, forms that present an object and owe an answer, not the post-war confession, which presents an answer and owes an object. Where I part from the other voice on this bench: a device demonstrated at the working unit is a true thing about the page and no defence at all against the apparatus, which read past this procedure for sixty years with the page open in front of it. The page was never the difficulty.

The poem's instruction is four words long and perfectly clear. The reception signed for everything.

**RETICLE:**

The evidence-first method this voice runs on would normally set the passage down whole before saying anything about it; source exposure holds the display here to phrase anchors, and I will work from the punctuation, which is where the procedure is anyway.

Consider the working unit. It is not the image and not the line. It is the interrogative mark, which appears at L1, L3, and L5 and never again, and the imperative mood, which appears at L2 and L4 and never again. Take the refusal at L2, four words, recurring at L4 with the order of its two sentences reversed. Those two marks together constitute a *priem*, a device in the Formalist sense of a procedure that does work: they convert each noun phrase into a candidate for processing, so that presentation and verdict become a two-step operation the reader learns in six lines.

What, then, is the function of this procedure? It is not to refuse three objects. Refusing three objects is what the procedure does while the reader is learning it. Its function is to establish a protocol so that the protocol's withdrawal can be felt. A noun phrase arriving at L7 arrives unprocessed, and the reader knows it is unprocessed because the reader has been trained for six lines to expect processing. The poem does not lay bare the device, which is the familiar move and the one that would be easy to praise. It installs the device and then removes it, which is the rarer operation, and the reading has to say which of the two it is looking at or it has said nothing.

That places the poem in a line that runs through the riddle, where an object is presented and an answer is owed, and through the emblem, where an image is presented with its verdict attached beneath it. The poem keeps the presentation and cancels the verdict, three times by refusal and then four times by silence. Both forms were available in 1962 and the placement needs no anachronism. The forward link, to later writing that makes procedure the whole content, is retrospective and is marked as such; it is not what the poem is doing and it is where I would overreach if I were not watching.

Where I part from the other voice: the drawer is not the object. A reassignment from one shelf to another is a claim about librarians, and a genealogy argued from reception facts rather than demonstrated at the working unit is positioning without evidence. The procedure is on the page, and the page answers for itself whether or not anybody read it. The poem is re-readable because the relation between the refused objects and the unrefused ones changes on every pass, and that relation is a fact about the punctuation, not about the 1965 table of contents.

I should also say where I cannot reach. This poet sits outside the line this voice ordinarily works, and the procedural reading I have given is the strongest claim the page supports rather than a placement in a tradition I can trace forward. The silence is data and is left visible.

Disagreement left standing. The two positions are not reconciled, and the Verdict field below does not reconcile them.
