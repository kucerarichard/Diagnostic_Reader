# CASANOVA EXTRACTION

Version: 0.6
Source: Pascale Casanova, *The World Republic of Letters*, 1999, English translation by M. B. DeBevoise, 2004.

A directive extension for the Portable Poetics Kernel v0.3. It carries five tools, one directive, one recorded challenge to the apparatus, and a ledger of what was examined and refused. It runs standalone and requires no private core.

## Module Manifest

Module name: Casanova Extraction
Module type: directive extension
Kernel requirement: Portable Poetics Kernel v0.3
Private-core requirement: none
Source status: in copyright. This module paraphrases and names concepts. It does not reproduce Casanova's prose.

Exports:

- triggers: none by keyword. Tools fire on the crossing test below
- procedures: position reading; meridian reading; consecration audit; littérarisation trace; cellar inversion
- output fields: position note, when a positional claim changes the reading
- constraints: no voice zone; no world-systems apparatus; no universal jurisdiction
- failure modes: sociologizing, center-confirmation, position-as-verdict

## Scope

Not a voice module. It opens no voice zone, and nothing here renders as Casanova speaking.

Not a mode. It adds no operating mode and no output form of its own.

The second extraction built on the same finding. Frye claimed synoptic jurisdiction over the literary system, Casanova claims that position explains everything including the cases that look furthest from determination. Take the mechanism, refuse the jurisdiction.

The voice test, the candidate log, and the weight of the evidence across candidates are in `0b-voice-slot-registry`.

## The Stand-Down She Does Not Supply

Casanova has no limiting condition, and I told you in an earlier session that she did. That was wrong. Her preface claims the method reaches not only texts touching the colonial or imperial question but works like Beckett's and Kafka's that appear furthest removed from any historical or political determination. The apparently autonomous case is where she plants her flag, not where she stops.

So the extraction constructs one, using her own stated criterion rather than an imported objection. She describes the book's sole purpose as proposing a reading tool that is at once internal and textual and external and historical, and she positions the argument against a French tradition of purely internal reading and against external literary history, calling the antinomy false.

**The crossing test.** A tool here fires when a positional claim can be cashed out in the words on the page. If knowing the position changes what you can see in the text, the tool has fired. If it only changes what you can say about the author's circumstances, the tool has not fired and the reading has drifted into sociology.

Apply it as a gate, not a suggestion:

> Before a position note enters the sidecar, name the textual feature it explains.

A position note with no feature attached is cut.

## Tool 1: Position

The core move is situating a work by its place in international literary space rather than inside a national tradition. Her claim is that literary studies is organized nationally almost everywhere, that this leaves a literary unconscious which is national by default, and that a set of transnational phenomena becomes invisible as a result.

For this apparatus the useful part is narrower than her claim. Position is a source of evidence about the text, available where the text bears marks of a crossing: a borrowed form, an imported procedure, a language not the writer's first, a translation history, a foreign consecration.

### The Beckett Demonstration

Worth recording because it is her strongest case, and the apparatus should meet the strong version.

She reads Beckett's radical autonomy as produced by position rather than in spite of it. An Irish writer in Paris, self-translating in both directions, and she treats the bilingual condition as the engine that made the challenge to received linguistic and narrative practice possible. She traces a sequence of breaks: with the nation politically, then with debates over national literary history, then with the aesthetic choices a national space dictates, then with language itself. The endpoint is *Worstward Ho*, which she describes as generating its own syntax and vocabulary and decreeing its own grammar.

The argument is serious and the apparatus should not answer it with a shrug. Note also the tension inside it: she calls the late work totally autonomous, referring to nothing but itself, and explains it entirely by position. Both claims are load-bearing for her and they pull against each other. That tension is where a reading using this tool is most likely to overreach.

## Tool 2: The Greenwich Meridian

Her most portable instrument, and it is a clock rather than a map.

Literary space needs a common measure of time, and the meridian is the point from which the literary present is determined. Aesthetic distance from the center gets measured temporally, as remove from whatever canons define the present at the moment of judgment. Her temporal law: a literature must be old to have any chance of being modern or of decreeing what is modern, since a long past is the condition of claiming a fully recognized present.

### What This Gives the Apparatus

A vocabulary of critical dismissal turns out to be positional measurement wearing the costume of description.

`Provincial`. `Dated`. `Belated`. `Anachronistic`. `Ahead of its time`. `Not yet arrived`. None of these describes a poem. Each reports a distance from a center, taken by a reader standing at that center, at a particular moment.

> When the reading reaches for a temporal judgment, name the center it is measuring from.

The measurement runs in both directions. She reads British reception of German writing as roughly forty years behind, which makes the receiving culture the belated one. Belatedness is not a property of peripheries.

Attaches to Checkpoint 2 period fit, to L3 in Genopersistation, and to Rother's reception work, which currently diagnoses inside the Anglophone anthology tradition and stops at the language border.

### Bounded Claim

She places Paris at the meridian and dates its hold to roughly the 1960s, describing the present as transitional toward a polycentric arrangement with London and New York foremost. So the Paris claim is historical and has an end date. Do not carry it forward as a standing fact, and do not treat the meridian as permanently located anywhere.

### Rival Centers

Her model is not monocentric even in its own period, and reading it that way is the commonest way to misuse it.

Her Brussels case: a rival capital offering a second chance to work the great capitals had rejected. Belgium's late and artificial creation in 1830 left it without the older antagonisms, and its cosmopolitan openness made it the capital of Symbolism and of Wagnerism outside Germany, welcoming Wagner when French anti-German feeling after 1870 had closed Paris to work from across the Rhine.

So the meridian question is not only where the center is but which of several centers did the reading, and rejection at one is not exclusion from all. When a tool here says name the center, it means name which one.

### The Two-Center Differential

Multiple centers are not only a caveat. They are an instrument, and this is the strongest test in the module.

When one work has been consecrated at two centers, compare the two readings. The divergence measures the receiving fields, not the work. Whatever the two accounts do not share was supplied by the readers, and the shape of what each supplied is a description of the internal disputes each was fighting at the time.

> Has this work been consecrated more than once? If so, what does the difference between the readings reveal about each reader?

Worked in full at Tool 6 under Ibsen.

## Tool 3: Consecration, and the Translation Index

Consecration is the mechanism by which a work becomes legible as literature: recognition by a center, through translation, commentary, critical notice, and prizes.

The part worth taking is that she makes it measurable. Translation share of total literary production is her index of a market's openness, and low share indicates a center that does not need to import.

Her figures, for 1990 and thereabouts: Britain at 3.3 percent, a European average near 15 percent, France and Germany between 14 and 18, Portugal at 33, Sweden at 60. She calls the Anglo-American markets close to autarkic and notes British resistance to foreign work as higher then than in the 1950s and 1960s.

### Why This Matters Here

The Anglophone anthology tradition that Rother's reception diagnosis works inside sits in a market importing around three percent. That is a structural fact about why the canon has the shape it has, and it is a number rather than an impression.

> When diagnosing an anthology's keep-list, ask what the receiving market was importing.

Figures are three decades old. Cite them as period evidence, not as current conditions.

## Tool 4: Littérisation

The operation by which a text acquires the status of literature.

Her definition in chapter 4 is explicit and covers a class rather than a single act: translation, self-translation, transcription, or direct composition in the dominant language, any of which can bring a text from a literarily deprived country to be regarded as literary by the legitimate authorities. She calls it a change of literary being rather than a transfer of meaning, and the point of the term is that the text does not merely move, it acquires a certificate.

### Terminology Note

The book carries two spellings and two senses, and they are not reconciled in it.

`Littérarisation`, chapter 2, is a **language** accruing literary value. Seventeenth-century French made into a literary language through grammars, academies, and public quarrels over single words.

`Littérisation`, chapter 4, is a **text** acquiring literary status. The contents page names that section with the first spelling and the body heading uses the second, so the inconsistency is the book's rather than the reader's.

Carry both. The language sense answers how a register or form came to be readable as literature at all. The text sense answers how a particular work got its certificate. Do not collapse them, and do not treat the difference as settled.

### The Four Directions

The operation means something different depending on which way it runs, and this grid is the usable part.

| Direction | What the operation does |
|---|---|
| Small target imports from the center | Gathers literary resources, diverts assets. Polyglot writers on the periphery import the center's modernity and perpetuate its domination by doing so. |
| Large source exports | Diffuses central capital and disseminates central aesthetic norms, with a delay, since translation takes time. |
| Large target imports from a small language | **Annexation.** Peripheral works get added to the stock of central resources. The consecrating translator discovers nonnative writers who suit the categories already in place. |
| Small source gets translated | The text accedes to literary status and obtains a certificate of standing. |

The third row is the one this apparatus already half-knows. Discovering writers who fit the categories already held is appropriation stated as a structural mechanism rather than an accusation, and it attaches directly to the appropriation risk in the Small Branch calibration and to Load-Bearing Foreignness, where an imported form arrives carrying value accumulated elsewhere.

Her instances of the fourth row: the Latin American boom existing internationally only after French translation, Borges calling himself an invention of France, Tagore's Nobel dating from his own translation of his work into English.

### The Bind Under the Fourth Row

Chapter 9 gives the row its cost. A writer in a small language chooses between translation into a literary language, which grants literary existence and cuts them off from their compatriots, and staying in the small language, which means invisibility or a purely national existence. Poets who convert to a great language are sometimes charged with treason. She treats this as a structural contradiction rather than a strategy, which is why the chapter is titled a tragedy.

The responses form a continuum rather than a menu, and a writer may move along it across a career: adopting the dominant language, self-translation, building a dual body of work by translating in both directions, promoting a national or popular language, or merging two languages into a new writing. Her named instances of that last one are usable as device names: Mário de Andrade's Brazilianization of Portuguese, Rabearivelo's Malagasy French, Achebe's Africanization of English, Darío's mental Gallicism.

### Language Positions

Small languages sort by literariness, and the position is an external fact the crossing test can cash out against the page.

| Position | Instances she gives |
|---|---|
| Oral, or script still being settled. No literary capital, no translation route | some African languages, some creoles now acquiring codified written form |
| Recently created or recreated, national after independence. Few speakers and works, must build international existence through translation | Catalan, Korean, Gaelic, Hebrew, new Norwegian |
| Ancient tradition attached to a small country. Real literary credit, unrecognized past its own borders | Dutch, Danish, Greek, Persian |
| Broad diffusion and great internal tradition, little international recognition | Arabic, Chinese, Hindi |

She also names the chronically translated language, one the central authorities can read only in translation because no polyglots read the original: Yoruba, Kikuyu, Amharic, Gaelic, Yiddish.

### Worked Case: The Portable Faulkner

Anthology consecration, which is Rother's territory with the border removed.

Faulkner was consecrated in Paris well before America. *The Sound and the Fury* sold under two thousand copies, and he stayed practically unknown at home for another fifteen years. Coindreau, Larbaud, Malraux, and Sartre did the French work through the thirties. Cowley's 1946 anthology *The Portable Faulkner* then won him American critical recognition and revived his sales, and she reads the Nobel as a consequence of the earlier Parisian consecration rather than of domestic reputation.

Two things for the apparatus. An anthology keep-list can manufacture a national reputation rather than reflect one. And a writer's home tradition may be the last to consecrate him, which means Selective Salvage should ask where the salvage was performed before asking what it kept.

## Tool 5: The Cellar Inversion

From her reading of Kafka's 1911 and 1912 diary notes on small literatures.

In a literature with little accumulated capital, what sits in the basement of a large literature comes up into daylight. Genre hierarchies invert, the boundary between private and collective concern shrinks so that individual subjects read as political, and the literature is popular in the sense of belonging to a people rather than to a literary history. Kafka's observation, which she flags as unusually perceptive, is that new national literatures are also popular literatures.

### Where It Bites Here

This is a structural account of why marginal material becomes central material under certain conditions, and it puts pressure on a loaded module.

The delight taken in petty themes is, on this account, a feature of a literature in a particular position, not a free aesthetic election. Kowit's whole program values the ordinary handled plainly. He treats it as an ethical choice against a priesthood. Casanova and Kafka would treat it as what a literature does when it lacks accumulated capital.

Both may be true and they are not the same claim. Record the confrontation as an axis in Triangulated Commentary rather than resolving it, and do not let the extraction, which has no voice zone, settle a question against a voice that does.

## Tool 6: Reception at the Center

Consecration is celebration and annexation inseparably, and chapter 4 gives two tests for telling how much of each is running. She calls the combined operation universalization through denial of difference: consecrating nations reduce foreign work to their own categories of perception, mistake those categories for universal norms, and drop the historical and literary context that made the work possible. She reads the result as a long history of productive misunderstanding rather than as occasional error.

### Test A: The Universality Audit

Her sharpest claim, and it reframes a whole vocabulary. Universality is the center's instrument. Proclaiming that all citizens of the republic of letters are equal denies the hierarchy that actually structures it, so the monopolists of universality command submission while announcing equality. What counts as universal is what they alone declare accessible to all.

`Universal`. `Timeless`. `Speaks to the human condition`. `Transcends its moment`. `Needs no context`. This is the standard vocabulary of anthology laundering, and Casanova supplies the mechanism under it. Attach to Anti-Laundering Engine Default.

Achebe's reversibility test makes it operational. He objected to a critic who found a Gambian novel universal on the ground that with a few substitutions it could pass for an American one, and asked whether it would ever occur to such a critic to run the swap in reverse on a Roth or an Updike. His point: a Western writer's work is taken to be informed by universality automatically, and only others must strain toward it.

> Would this universality claim be made in the other direction?

Marthe Robert on the French Kafka states the cost precisely. Dehistoricized, granted a right of extraterritoriality, coming from nowhere and belonging to everyone, he was accorded a purity available only to abstract things, in exchange for his real existence. That is Selective Salvage with the price named: aesthetic perfection bought with historical existence.

### Test B: The Distance Rule

Recognition requires a writer to sit at a particular distance from the judges. Different enough to be noticed, not so different as to be invisible. Neither too near nor too far, and the admissible band is narrow.

Ramuz stayed imperceptible while writing as though he belonged to French letters and became visible only after declaring himself a Swiss writer from Vaud. Cassou, in 1924, described the French demand that foreigners surprise them in a manner the French were already prepared to specify. Crémazie, writing as a French Canadian, observed that work in Huron or Iroquoian would fascinate Europe while the same book written in French by a colonist of Quebec would not find fifty readers.

This is where literary exoticism comes from, and it extends the annexation row of Tool 4's grid. The center wants difference, and it wants difference it has pre-specified. Attaches to the appropriation risk in the Small Branch calibration, which now has a mechanism rather than a caution.

### Worked Case: Larbaud, Boyd, and Joyce

Both sides are right, which is what makes it usable.

Larbaud consecrated *Ulysses* in Paris. The Irish critic Ernest Boyd attacked him for ignorance of Irish literature and of the Anglo-Irish writers, reading his remarks as an attack on Irish literary identity. Larbaud replied that his merit was being the first outside the English domain to call Joyce a great writer, at a moment when nobody in Ireland had said it.

Casanova reads the exchange as the national view of literature against the dehistoricizing impulse. Paris ennobled and internationalized the book and ignored everything that made it possible. Her formulation: the denationalized capital denationalized texts to fit its own conception of literary art.

Use this when a reading has to hold a consecration that was both a rescue and a flattening. The apparatus should not have to pick.

### Worked Case: Ibsen in Two Capitals

Her only sustained case of one writer received by two centers, and it does more work than the Joyce case.

Ibsen was consecrated in London as a realist handling concrete social problems, and in Paris during the same years as a Symbolist conveying universal poetic insight. Same plays, opposite readings, both successful.

Neither reading came from the plays. Each came from a fight inside the receiving field. Shaw, an Irishman who had arrived in London penniless from Dublin at twenty, needed a weapon against Victorian propriety and the absence of social criticism on the English stage, and read Ibsen alongside Wagner as a fellow heretical foreigner useful for that purpose. In Paris, Antoine had already staged Ibsen as a naturalist at the Théâtre-Libre, so Lugné-Poe made him a Symbolist at the Théâtre de l'Oeuvre precisely in order to establish himself as the innovator against a naturalism then in decline. The Symbolist Ibsen was generated by a rivalry between two Paris theaters.

Casanova's mechanism is worth stating exactly, because it generalizes past Ibsen. Discoverers profess to present a work on its own terms. What they do is exploit the foreign author's weakness in local literary politics to modify the work in ways that strengthen their own standing. An imported work is defenceless in the receiving field's disputes, which is what makes it useful in them.

Three further findings from the case:

**Consented annexation.** Ibsen called Paris the real heart of the world and reluctantly accepted the Symbolist reading as the price of fame, while still insisting on reviewing translations and performance details. Authorial acquiescence in a misreading is not evidence for the misreading, and this is the Frye extraction's interest-and-authority line applied to reception rather than to composition.

**Self-reproduction.** She notes that French critics were still calling Ibsen a Symbolist at the time of writing, repeating the previous century's readings. An annexation installs itself and then runs on its own, which is what Selective Salvage describes at L3.

**The diagnostician inside the mechanism.** Shaw named English ethnocentrism accurately, observing that the English could only reach for a modern Shakespeare looming in Scandinavia because Shakespeare's supremacy had taught them each national literature has one dominant figure. Maeterlinck got the same treatment as the Belgian Shakespeare. Shaw both performed the annexation and diagnosed it, which is the honest position for any reading using these tools.

One layer underneath, easy to miss and useful. Ibsen wrote *The League of Youth* in imitation of Scribe and Dumas fils, importing French dramatic technique in order to build a Norwegian style free of German tutelage. That is Tool 4's first row: a peripheral writer importing the center's modernity to escape a different domination. Paris later read a Symbolism back into work he had reached partly by way of French realism.

## Tool 7: Difficulty and Distance

Not a passage in the book. A consequence of Tool 1 applied to the reader instead of the writer, and it earns a place because it patches a blind spot in a loaded module.

Difficulty that turns out to be distance from a dispute is not the same as difficulty in the work.

### Three Sources

**Operational.** The poem's own devices resist. Ellipsis, dense syntax, unresolved ambiguity, withheld referents. It lives in the text and survives any amount of context.

**Positional.** The poem addresses a dispute, a community, or a body of reference the reader is not inside. It lives in the gap between the reader and the audience the poem assumes, not in the poem. Entering the dispute dissolves it.

**Gating.** The poem manufactures difficulty as a credential. This is Kowit's cult-mimicry diagnostic, and it lives in the poem's posture rather than in its operations.

### The Test

> Would one external fact dissolve this difficulty?

If yes, it is positional, and the reading's job is to name the dispute rather than to describe a device that is not there. If the difficulty survives full context, it is operational or gating, and Engine vs. Residue separates those: operational difficulty does work, gating difficulty does not.

### Why This Patches Kowit

Cult-mimicry cannot currently tell gating from positional difficulty, and the two look identical from outside. A poem written for a specific quarrel is not gating anyone. It is simply not addressed to you, and reading it as a credential-claim is the reader's position mistaken for the poem's posture.

The apparatus already has the adjacent machinery in Access-Asymmetry Mode, which holds that a poem may operate differently for readers with and without the needed key. This tool adds the diagnostic question that mode does not ask: whether the missing key is a fact about a dispute or a feature of the poem.

### It Runs Both Ways

Accessibility is as position-relative as difficulty. A canonical poem is easy for readers inside its tradition, and that ease is an artifact of position exactly as the difficulty was.

So the tool fires on the friendly case too, which is where the apparatus is least defended. A reading that credits a poem with being reachable, when the reachability is the reader's position rather than the poem's achievement, has made Frye's direct-correlation error. Both halves of that pair now have a mechanism.

## Directive: Decline-Claims Are Position-Taking

The strongest methodological move in the book and it is portable without any of her apparatus.

Since a center's authority exists only through belief in it, an announcement that a center is declining is a move in the competition rather than an observation about the world. Her instruction to the analyst is to refuse such claims the status of truth they assert for themselves, and to describe how they are used and how effective they are.

Applies directly to the apparatus's own material. `The novel is dead`. `Poetry has lost its audience`. `Nobody reads verse anymore`. `Formalism is back`. `The MFA ruined American poetry`. Each is a bid, and the diagnostic question is who gains standing if it is believed.

She applies it to herself in the same passage, conceding that she is not indifferent, being the author of a book about those who manufacture universality and whose authority is now contested. Any use of this directive carries the same obligation.

> Whose position improves if this claim about the state of literature is accepted?

## Live Challenge: Radical Monadology

Recorded at the operator's instruction as a standing objection to the apparatus rather than as a directive inside it.

Casanova's introduction attacks what she calls radical monadology: treating each work as unique, irreducible, and measurable only against itself, which leaves literary history a random succession of singularities. Her Irish chapter states it flatly, that no literary project, not even the most formalistic, can be explained monadically, and that every project must be set against the totality of rival projects in the same space.

This apparatus is monadological in exactly that sense. It reads one specimen at a time and builds a sidecar around it. The challenge is aimed at the architecture, not at a directive within it.

### The Term Misfires

She never names Leibniz and never engages the *Monadology*. Her uses of the word are all polemical: literatures sealed behind national borders like monads containing their own causality, and formalist projects explained monadically.

A Leibnizian monad is windowless and it mirrors the entire universe from its own position. Reflecting the whole system from one point of view is the definition rather than the defect. So the epithet inverts the term it borrows, and the inverted version happens to describe what this apparatus attempts: read one specimen closely enough and the system becomes legible in it.

That is a reply rather than a refutation. Whether a single poem can in fact carry the system, or whether the apparatus only assumes it can, remains open and is worth testing rather than assuming.

### Standing Status

Open. Do not resolve it in a reading. When a sidecar's findings turn out to depend on a comparison the apparatus never made, that is evidence for her side, and it should be logged rather than smoothed.

## Refusal Ledger

**The world-systems apparatus.** She names her borrowings, Braudel's economy-world and Bourdieu's field. Neither comes in. The tools above work without them, and importing two more theoretical vocabularies triggers Apparatus Bloom for no diagnostic gain.

**Paris as the permanent meridian.** Refused as a standing fact and kept as a dated historical claim, per her own qualification.

**Total positional explanation.** Her claim that position accounts for even the most autonomous work is the jurisdiction claim, and it is what the crossing test exists to bound. The tools stay; the claim does not.

**The quarrel with Deleuze and Guattari over minor literature.** She holds that their reading deforms Kafka's meaning by resting on a single diary entry. Refused as out of scope. This apparatus has no stake in it, and importing the dispute imports two more positions to adjudicate.

## Failure Modes

### Sociologizing

The reading describes the author's position in a field and never returns to the words.

Correction: apply the crossing test. Name the textual feature the position explains, or cut the note.

### Center-Confirmation

The analysis reproduces the hierarchy it set out to expose, because the vocabulary of center and periphery makes the center the reference point in every sentence.

Correction: run the measurement in the other direction, since a receiving culture can be the belated one. Then run the universality audit on the reading's own language, because the vocabulary of the universal is the center's instrument and a reading can pick it up without noticing.

### Elective and Imposed Conflation

Two writers work in a language not their first, the formal operation looks identical on the page, and the reading treats them as the same case.

Casanova refuses this explicitly. Cioran, Kundera, and Beckett adopted French, and Strindberg did so for a time, as routes out of peripheral invisibility and out of the national norms governing their own spaces. For writers from long-colonized countries, and she says for them alone, bilingualism is the primary and indelible mark of political domination, with Memmi's account of a mother tongue humiliated to the point that the writer comes to share the contempt.

Correction: before naming a language operation, establish whether the second language arrived by election or by conquest. The mechanism may be the same and the diagnosis is not. Kateb Yacine's account of writing in French in order to tell the French he is not French is not assimilation, and reading it as assimilation is the error this guard exists to catch.

### Position-As-Verdict

Positional standing gets treated as a finding about quality. A poem consecrated by a center is not thereby good, and a poem outside every circuit is not thereby neglected genius.

Correction: this is the Frye extraction's Pair 1, merit and reception, with the same two-sided guard.

## Access Honesty

This extraction rests on roughly nineteen thousand words of a hundred and forty-four thousand.

The tragedy of translated men was flagged at v0.2, extended the fourth row as predicted, and additionally supplied the elective-and-imposed guard, which nothing else in the module had.

Four predictions have now been made against this ledger and all four held. It also supplied the local-dispute mechanism, consented annexation, and the self-reproduction of an installed misreading.

Remaining unread: chapter 2 in substance, the language games and being universal sections of chapter 4, the assimilated and the rebels, the remainder of chapter 9, the Irish paradigm apart from its closing page, and most of the revolutionaries chapter. The assimilated and the rebels is now the likeliest to add something, since it probably populates the Tool 4 continuum with further positions, but the yield curve has flattened. Four rounds have each corrected something; a fifth may not.
