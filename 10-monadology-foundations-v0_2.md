## Scope

Not a voice module. Leibniz wrote nothing about poems.

Not an extraction. It yields no diagnostic tools, and a foundations note that produced tools would be an extraction misfiled.

What it does: name the structure the apparatus already assumes, so that three disciplines currently held as good practice can be held as consequences instead. Access Honesty, the reconstruction-strength grades, and Triangulated Commentary's refusal to resolve disagreement all follow from one premise, and this note states the premise.

## The Charge, and Its Precedent

The Casanova extraction records a live challenge. She attacks radical monadology, meaning the treatment of each work as unique, irreducible, and measurable only against itself, which leaves literary history a random succession of singularities. Her Irish chapter states it flatly: no literary project, not even the most formalistic, can be explained monadically, and every project must be set against the totality of rival projects in the same space.

Her epithet is borrowed and she never engages the source. Latta's Appendix F records that the objection had already been made to Leibniz himself, by Pierre Bayle in the *Dictionary* article on Rorarius, and Leibniz answered it.

Bayle's version, transposed: a simple indivisible substance with no external inputs cannot generate the variety it displays, since it lacks the instruments. A machine of several pieces can vary because each piece alters the others, and in an independent simple substance there is nowhere to locate the cause of variety.

The structure is identical to Casanova's. A single thing cannot be explained by itself, so the explanation must be external.

Leibniz's reply in his 1702 paper turns on the singer reading at sight. The whole score of the books to be sung is potentially graven in the soul from the beginning of its existence, and the soul cannot be conscious of it because it is enveloped in the soul's confused perceptions, which express all the detail of the universe. His conclusion is that the soul does have every instrument Bayle demanded, arranged as required, and that they are not material instruments but the preceding perceptions themselves, from which the succeeding ones arise.

The reply is not that the single thing is self-sufficient. It is that the whole is already present in it, mostly below the threshold of resolution, and that what looks like a missing external cause is an internal state not yet distinct.

This does not settle the literary question. It establishes that the objection is three centuries old, that it has a structural answer, and that Casanova reached for the name of the position that answers her.

## Whole in Part

Two sections carry it.

Section 56: each simple substance has relations expressing all the others, and is therefore a perpetual living mirror of the universe.

Section 60: nothing can confine a monad to representing only one part of things, since its nature is to represent.

This is the premise under single-specimen diagnosis. The apparatus reads one poem and expects the system to be legible in it, and that expectation is either an assumption or a structural claim. Here it is the claim: the whole is expressed in each part, so a sufficiently close reading of one specimen is not a sample of the system but an expression of it.

## The Confusion Clause

Section 60 gives the limit in the same breath as the licence, and the limit is the more useful half.

The representation is confused as regards the detail of the whole universe and can be distinct only as regards a small part, namely what is nearest or greatest in relation to each monad. Leibniz's reason: otherwise each monad would be a deity.

Then the formulation that does the work. The monads are limited not as regards their object but as regards the different ways in which they have knowledge of their object. Every monad's object is the whole. What differs is resolution.

Three existing disciplines follow from this rather than from prudence.

**Access Honesty.** Declaring what was read and what was not is not modesty. It is a report on the resolution available to this reading. A reading that omits the declaration is claiming distinct knowledge of the whole, which the premise reserves.

**Reconstruction strength.** The grades of strong, weak, and asymmetric are resolution measurements. Asymmetric is the ordinary case rather than a hedge, since distinctness is uneven by construction and clusters on what is nearest or greatest.

**The Mixed Log.** Holding hypotheses open, and closing one only on stated grounds, is what confused expression requires. Early closure claims a distinctness the reading does not have.

Section 83 keeps this from becoming quietism. Minds, as distinct from souls in general, are images of the author of nature and are capable of knowing the system of the universe and to some extent imitating it. So partial system-knowledge is available to a creature. The apparatus may attempt the whole. It may not claim to have resolved it.

## Distinct and Confused as Active and Passive

The unexpected find, and the closest thing here to a tool, which is why the note stops short of making it one.

Section 49: activity is attributed to a monad in so far as it has distinct perceptions, and passivity in so far as its perceptions are confused.

Section 50: one created thing is more perfect than another in that the more perfect contains what serves to explain a priori what takes place in the less perfect, and on that account the former is said to act upon the latter.

Latta's Appendix C, from a 1686 draft letter to Arnauld, makes the consequence explicit. Activity is attributed to the substance whose expression is the more distinct, and that substance is what we call the cause. His illustration is a body moving through water: one may equally suppose the body at rest and everything else moving, and the reason for attributing the motion to the body is that by its means what happens can be explained distinctly. His constraint on that freedom matters as much as the freedom: taking all the phenomena, great and small, there is only one hypothesis which explains the whole distinctly.

Read against Engine vs. Residue, this describes what the apparatus does when it names an engine. The engine is the locus at which the poem's operation becomes distinctly explicable, and naming it is a causal attribution in exactly Leibniz's sense. The residue is what remains confused, and calling it residue is a claim about resolution rather than about worth.

The constraint is the part worth keeping. Distinctness is not a matter of taste, because only one hypothesis explains the whole distinctly. That is the Mixed Log's dominant-hypothesis rule with a reason attached, and it is why a reading can be wrong rather than merely unfashionable.

Left as a foundation and not promoted to a tool, because Engine vs. Residue already has its procedure and this adds an account of why the procedure works rather than a new gate.

## Perspective Is Not Averaging

Section 57: the same town, looked at from various sides, appears quite different and becomes numerous in aspects, so that the infinite number of simple substances is as if there were many different universes, which are nothing but perspectives of a single universe according to each monad's point of view.

Section 9 supplies the individuation: no two monads are alike, and there is always an internal difference or a difference founded on an intrinsic quality.

Put with section 60, the inference is available though Leibniz does not put it in these words: what individuates a monad is which parts of the whole it renders distinctly. Mark that as inference rather than text.

Triangulated Commentary follows. Three voices on one specimen are not three partial views converging toward a single correct account. They are three orderings of the same whole, differing in what each resolves, and merging them destroys exactly what distinguishes them. Disagreement is the output because perspective is constitutive rather than accidental.

That is also why the consensus-smoothing flag exists. Averaging the voices produces a reading that has lost the only thing the multiplicity was for. The flag has been a house rule and is now a consequence.

## Not a Hologram

The analogy fails twice, and the failures are instructive.

A hologram needs a viewer outside the plate. The whole scene is recoverable from a fragment, but only by illuminating it and inspecting the reconstruction. There is no outside for monads, since any vantage would itself be a monad, and God's distinct perception is the ground of the system rather than an external observation of it.

A hologram encodes the same scene at every point, so a fragment yields the whole at uniformly degraded resolution. Expression is not uniform. What is distinct in one monad is confused in another, and the pattern of what resolves is what individuates it.

The difference is operational. On the hologram reading, one specimen gives a blurry copy of the system and more specimens would sharpen it, which makes specimens redundant and Casanova right that the single case is a poor instrument. On the reading above, the specimen's particular distribution of clarity and confusion is itself the finding, and no other specimen's distribution substitutes for it.

## No Windows, and the Limit of the Point

Section 7: there is no way of explaining how a monad can be altered in quality or internally changed by any other created thing, and the monads have no windows through which anything could come in or go out.

Section 51: the influence of one monad upon another is only ideal and takes effect only through the mediation of God, since no created monad can have physical influence upon the inner being of another.

Within the system, external causation is therefore ruled out and correspondence substitutes for it. Casanova's method is external causation throughout: position explains the work. So the two candidates once considered for the same voice slot turn out to be metaphysical opposites.

The point stops there, and this is the honest bound. Section 51 makes God the mediator of all apparent influence, and that is load-bearing rather than decorative. The apparatus cannot use no-windows against Casanova without buying the theology that makes it work. What the collision establishes is that the two positions are incompatible at the root, not that Leibniz refutes her. Her mechanisms remain usable, and the Casanova extraction stands unchanged.

## Why Not a Voice

Two reasons, either sufficient.

He wrote nothing about poems. A voice module requires declared recurring moves, an operational lexicon, output zones, and worked examples on specimens, and the three loaded voices each stand on a corpus of actual readings. Building this one would mean inventing a Leibnizian critic, and the invention would be the builder's rather than Leibniz's.

Period fit fails, and fails in a way that closes the loop. The *Monadology* is 1714, and Casanova's second chapter dates the invention of literature in the modern sense to roughly that period. A voice that cannot recognize its object is not a voice.

## The Reopen Condition, Amended

The whole-system voice slot is closed, and its reopen condition originally ran as a thinker who holds the whole-system view and can state where the method does not apply. The condition now lives in `0b-voice-slot-registry`.

Leibniz satisfies that as written. The confusion clause is a genuine stand-down, stated by the system rather than imposed on it, and it is stronger than the limits Frye or Casanova offer. He remains unusable.

So the condition was underspecified and needed a further clause: the method must have purchase on poems. That amendment, and its history, are recorded in the registry. The defect was found by testing the condition against a case it was not written for, which is the only way such conditions get tested.

## Refusal Ledger

**The theology.** God as mediator of all influence, the proof of the existence of God in Latta's Appendix G, the City of God, and the harmony of the realms of nature and grace. All out. Section 51 shows this is not a detachable layer, which is why the no-windows point above is bounded rather than deployed.

**Optimism and the best of all possible worlds.** Out. It is an argument about the goodness of what exists and the apparatus makes no such claim about poems.

**The hierarchy of monads.** Bare monads, souls, spirits. Out, as a ranking scheme with no purchase on texts and an obvious route to Automatic Position.

**Pre-established harmony as doctrine.** Out. Latta's Appendix A works it through a pin-prick, and the apparatus needs the structure of expression rather than an account of mind and body.

## Failure Modes

### Metaphysical Laundering

The apparatus cites a 1714 metaphysics as though it validated a reading, and a house rule acquires borrowed authority it has not earned. This is the failure this module is most likely to cause, and it is the same operation the Anti-Laundering Engine catches elsewhere.

Correction: nothing here makes any reading correct. A structural analogy explains why a discipline hangs together. It supplies no evidence about any poem, and a sidecar that cites Leibniz in support of a finding has laundered the finding.

### Citation Without Verification

Section numbers get quoted from memory and drift. Sections 50 and 62 do not parse cleanly as separate blocks in this edition, and 62 was not read here.

Correction: verify against the text before citing a number. Latta's numbering follows Boutroux.

### Hologram Slippage

The analogy returns because it is convenient, bringing an external observer and uniform resolution with it, and the second of those quietly concedes Casanova's point.

Correction: see Not a Hologram.

## Access Honesty

Read in full: sections 7, 9, 13, 14, 49, 50, 51, 56, 57, 60, 61, 63, 83, and Latta's Appendices C and F.

Not read: the bulk of the ninety sections, Latta's introduction and prefatory note, and Appendices A, B, D, E, and G. Appendix E, on Kant's relation to Leibniz, is unread and is the likeliest place to find a limit on the whole-in-part premise, since Kant's objection is precisely that a finite intellect cannot have the object this premise assigns it.

The sections quoted above were extracted and read rather than recalled. The single inference marked as inference, that the pattern of distinctness individuates, rests on sections 9 and 60 together and is not stated by Leibniz in those terms.
