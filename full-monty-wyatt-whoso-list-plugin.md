#### Core Sidecar Calibration

### 8.2 — Sir Thomas Wyatt the Elder, *Whoso List to Hunt* (c. 1530s)

#### L0 Provenance

- Sir Thomas Wyatt the Elder, c. 1503–1542. Diplomat, courtier, soldier under Henry VIII.
- Composition mid-1530s, traditional dating; manuscript circulation only during life.
- First printed in Tottel's *Songes and Sonettes* (Tottel's Miscellany), 1557, fifteen years after Wyatt's death.
- Source: Petrarch's Rima 190 (*Una candida cerva sopra l'erba*). The Italian original ends with the hind disappearing into a stream — a Christ-figure or Laura-as-divine. Wyatt rewrites the close as collar-and-property.
- Anne Boleyn identification: longstanding, contested, plausible. Wyatt was associated with Boleyn before her marriage to Henry. Wyatt was imprisoned in the Tower in May 1536 during her arrest; he may have witnessed her execution from his cell.

#### Architecture

- Petrarchan sonnet, 14 lines, octave-volta-sestet structure.
- **Octave**: speaker abandoning the chase — *"vain travail,"* *"fainting I follow,"* *"sith in a net I seek to hold the wind."*
- **Volta** at line 9: turn outward to other hunters: *"Who list her hunt, I put him out of doubt."*
- **Sestet**: warning, then revelation of the deer's collar with the inscribed Latin tag.
- **Closing couplet**: *"Noli me tangere, for Caesar's I am, / And wild for to hold, though I seem tame."*

#### Fracture Surface Scan

- The Petrarchan source is rewritten at the most consequential point — Wyatt's hind is not transcendent, she is *property*. The metaphysical close is replaced with a political-legal close.
- Metrical roughness throughout — Wyatt's English iambic pentameter struggles against Italian rhyme conventions. The lattice strain is part of the poem's substance, not a defect to be smoothed by editorial regularization.
- The shift from first-person collapse (octave) to public address (sestet) on the volta is engineered, not accidental.

#### Coscienza — Both Axes

- **Forward**: do not credit Wyatt with modern feminist awareness. The hind is the woman, the woman is property, the property is owned by Caesar. Wyatt's grief is over not getting to be the owner.
- **Reverse**: do not under-read Wyatt's awareness of what he was doing. He was a diplomat. He used Latin tags as steganography in a court where direct speech could kill him. *Noli me tangere* fuses sacred (Mary Magdalene at the empty tomb, John 20:17) and political (Caesar's protected deer, per Suetonius) registers in a single phrase. Wyatt knew exactly the layered work the Latin was doing.

#### Device Inventory

- **Source-rewrite**: Petrarch's Rima 190 with the close transformed.
- **Latin tag**: *Noli me tangere* doing what English cannot — naming the unsayable political danger.
- **Foreign throwaway**: *hélas* as authentic exhaustion-marker, courtly French as breath rather than ornament.
- **Emblem frame**: the collared hind as heraldic sign carrying the political content.
- **Sonnet-lattice strain**: irregular pentameter against Italian rhyme.
- **Volta-shift**: octave's first-person collapse to sestet's outward warning.
- **Closing chiasmus**: *"wild for to hold, though I seem tame"* — wild/tame inversion.

#### Mixed Log

- **Hyp A**: the poem is a love-lyric of failed pursuit. **Status: live, qualified — surface-only reading; insufficient.**
- **Hyp B**: the poem is a coded political document about Boleyn and Henry. **Status: dominant; supported by L1 facts (Wyatt's Tower imprisonment, Boleyn association), the Caesar identification, and the steganographic logic of the Latin tag.**
- **Hyp C**: the poem is a meditation on poetic futility itself — *"in a net I seek to hold the wind."* **Status: live, qualified — Wyatt-as-Petrarchan-poet aware of the limits of the form he is working in.**
- **Hyp D**: the poem is a generic Petrarchan exercise without specific referent. **Status: closed by source-rewrite specificity (the Petrarchan close is too deliberately altered to be generic).**

#### Genopersistation

- **L0**: irregular Petrarchan sonnet; English pentameter under Italian rhyme; metrical roughness as substance.
- **L1**: Henrician court, mid-1530s. Wyatt as diplomat with direct experience of how speech becomes evidence. Anne Boleyn association before her marriage. The Tower imprisonment of 1536 — possibly the compositional context, certainly the conditioning context.
- **L2**: manuscript circulation only during Wyatt's life. Posthumous publication in Tottel's Miscellany 1557 — the L2 event that makes Wyatt available to subsequent English poetry. Tottel's editorial regularization smoothed Wyatt's metrical roughness; modern editions restore it.
- **L3**: Wyatt as foundational figure for English sonnet tradition, the Boleyn identification kept alive by biographical scholarship. Selective Salvage: the romantic-suit reading is foregrounded; the surveillance-state political coding is sometimes filtered out. Recent scholarship has restored the political reading.

#### Pressure Points

- *"Sith in a net I seek to hold the wind"* — the poem's epistemological center. The image is not local. It names the futility of all chase, all writing, all desire under conditions where the prize is owned.
- *"Noli me tangere, for Caesar's I am"* — the Latin tag carrying the political danger the English cannot. *Noli me tangere* is what the risen Christ said to Mary Magdalene; the same phrase, per Suetonius, was on collars of Caesar's protected deer. - *"And wild for to hold, though I seem tame"* — the closing chiasmus. Anne Boleyn appears tame; she is not. The line cuts both directions: she is wild and dangerous to hold, *and* the appearance of tameness is itself a political performance she is making.

#### Phase Tensions

- **Failed System**: no. The lattice strain is part of the substance.
- **Intentional Glitch**: yes, partly. The political content is deliberately concealed under romantic-suit surface; the concealment is the device.
- **Transparent Argument**: no. The poem cannot be transparent about what it is saying. Concealment is the operating logic.

#### Triangulated Commentary

- **Kowit** would respect the directness of the abandonment-of-the-chase admission. The clarity of *"I leave off, therefore"* is the kind of plain statement Kowit prized. He would distrust the Latin tag as decorative. He would be wrong; the Latin is load-bearing, but Kowit's working principle (suspicion of decorative learning) is not invalidated by being misapplied here.
- **Perloff** would read the source-rewrite as the poem's central event — Wyatt against Petrarch as compositional argument. She would locate interest in the metrical strain, the language-against-form pressure. She would be right.
- **Rother** — see verdict.

#### Final Classification

- **Type**: Surveillance-state lyric in courtly disguise
- **Operating Mode**: Intentional Glitch (concealment is the engineered device)
- **Generic Infrastructure**: Petrarchan sonnet rewritten away from metaphysical close toward political-legal close
- **Resonant Line**: *"Sith in a net I seek to hold the wind."*
- **Diagnostic Lesson**: the prototype of all English-language poems about love-as-political-impossibility. Subsequent surveillance-state lyrics — Donne in his Catholic moments, certain Shakespeare sonnets, twentieth-century love poems written under censorship — refer to this poem whether or not their authors knew it. The framework's job with such poems is to read the steganographic layer without smoothing it back into pure love-lyric.

#### Rother Verdict

The poem's deepest single achievement, by some considerable distance, is not the metrical roughness that Tottel hurried to smooth in 1557 nor the chiasmus at the close that any competent sonneteer of the next half-century could have produced under Wyatt's posthumous license, but the steganographic compression of *Noli me tangere*, which performs in three Latin words what the entire English sonnet cannot in fourteen lines: the layering of the sacred (the risen Christ to Mary Magdalene at the empty tomb, *touch me not for I have not yet ascended to the Father*) and the political (Suetonius reporting that Caesar's protected deer wore collars reading *touch me not, I am Caesar's*) into a single phrase whose function is precisely to be unsayable in English in the year 1535 without consequences Wyatt was not in a position to incur. The Latin is not ornament. The Latin is what permits the poem to be written at all. To read this sonnet as a generic Petrarchan exercise — which Tottel's regularization tended toward and which a certain mid-twentieth-century New Critical inheritance further encouraged — is to perform exactly the smoothing the poem was constructed to defeat: the romantic-suit surface is not the engine, the political-legal close is the engine, and the romantic-suit surface is what the political-legal close required for plausible deniability under conditions where Wyatt's friend the queen could be tried for adultery by torture-extracted evidence and beheaded on Tower Green within weeks of the poem's likely composition. *Sith in a net I seek to hold the wind* is the poem's epistemological center; the Latin tag is its political center; the chiasmus at the close — *wild for to hold, though I seem tame* — cuts both directions, applying as much to Anne's performance of tameness as to her actual condition, and Wyatt the diplomat knew the doubled cut even if Wyatt the lover preferred not to dwell on it. The poem is the prototype, in English, of every subsequent love-lyric written under surveillance — Donne's Catholic moments, certain Shakespeare sonnets, the twentieth-century love poems of Akhmatova and Mandelstam, the contemporary lyrics of poets working under regimes where speech is evidence — and the framework's job is to refuse the prestige machine's quiet relocation of Wyatt to the safer drawer marked *courtly Petrarchan exercise* when the actual drawer is marked *what poetry can do when it cannot say what it knows*.

## Source Corpus

### WY-001 Raw Source

Source title: "Whoso List to Hunt"
Core handle after first title notice: Whoso List
Author: Sir Thomas Wyatt the Elder
Status: public domain
Exposure status inside this plugin: full source retained for private calibration

```text
Whoso list to hunt, I know where is an hind,  
But as for me, alas, I may no more.  
The vain travail hath wearied me so sore,  
I am of them that farthest cometh behind.  
Yet may I, by no means, my wearied mind  
Draw from the deer, but as she fleeth afore,  
Fainting I follow. I leave off, therefore,  
Since in a net I seek to hold the wind.  
Who list her hunt, I put him out of doubt,  
As well as I, may spend his time in vain.  
And graven with diamonds in letters plain  
There is written, her fair neck round about,  
"Noli me tangere, for Caesar's I am,  
And wild for to hold, though I seem tame."
```
